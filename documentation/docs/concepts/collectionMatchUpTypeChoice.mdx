## Specification status

This is the proposed extension to the [tieFormat specification](./tieFormat.mdx), recorded on
`explore/noc-u4-collection-matchup-type`. It is not implemented or released. The branch's current conversion
prototype changes concrete SINGLES/DOUBLES collections; it does not yet recognize the declaration below,
generate unresolved choice rubbers, or enforce their restrictions. Do not submit this shape to an existing
release and assume that an ignored field means support.

## Declaration and selection are different facts

A Captain's Choice collection permits a choice of match type for one TEAM encounter. The declaration
belongs to the collection definition; the selected type belongs to that collection in the particular encounter.
Selection must preserve the declaration so consumers can distinguish a chosen singles collection from a
collection that was always singles.

Add optional `matchUpTypeOptions` to `collectionDefinition`. Initially its members are `SINGLES` and
`DOUBLES`. `CHOICE` is not a match type: scoring, participant cardinality and results still require a concrete
`matchUpType`. "Captain's Choice" is a collection name/presentation, not a new matchUpType constant.

### Unresolved declaration on an event, draw or structure

```json
{
  "collectionId": "captains-choice",
  "collectionName": "Captain's Choice",
  "matchUpTypeOptions": ["SINGLES", "DOUBLES"],
  "matchUpCount": 1,
  "matchUpFormat": "SET3-S:6/TB7-F:TB10",
  "matchUpValue": 1
}
```

There is deliberately no `matchUpType`. Neither the first option nor a stored DOUBLES placeholder is a
selection or an implicit default. Existing collections with a concrete `matchUpType` and no options retain
their fixed-type meaning.

### Resolved declaration on a TEAM encounter

The encounter's effective tieFormat contains the same collection, with the concrete selection added:

```json
{
  "collectionId": "captains-choice",
  "collectionName": "Captain's Choice",
  "matchUpTypeOptions": ["SINGLES", "DOUBLES"],
  "matchUpType": "SINGLES",
  "matchUpCount": 1,
  "matchUpFormat": "SET3-S:6/TB7-F:TB10",
  "matchUpValue": 1
}
```

Another encounter may select DOUBLES from the same template. Selection writes an encounter-specific
copy/override of the effective tieFormat using existing inline or centralized identity rules; it does not
edit a shared template in place. A centralized fork uses an origin-supplied ID pool for deterministic replay.

## Validation rules

- An unresolved choice has options and no concrete type; a resolved choice has both. A fixed collection has
  a concrete type and no options. A collection with neither is invalid for new use.
- Options must be an array of at least two distinct, recognized concrete types. Initial support permits only
  SINGLES and DOUBLES. Reject an empty list, duplicates, unknown types, TEAM, HYBRID and CHOICE. Preserve
  option order for presentation; do not infer meaning or a default from that order.
- A selected `matchUpType` must be a member of the options. Resolve choices only at parent TEAM matchUp
  scope in this first version. Templates carrying options must remain unresolved; broad preselection and
  event/draw/structure-wide conversion require a separate design.
- Every option shares the collection's count, scoring format, gender/category constraints and value model.
  Validate each permitted combination when the declaration is created, and validate the selected combination
  again on resolution. MIXED doubles cannot be offered alongside MIXED singles under the existing gender
  rules. This version does not define different genders, scoring formats or values for different options.
- A selection applies to every collectionPosition in that encounter's collection and to both sides. Independent
  per-position choices, or different types for the opposing teams, are outside this contract.

The declaration states what can be chosen. Which captain has authority, how captains agree, eligibility,
submission deadlines and permissions remain competition/application policy. This field does not grant
permission to choose or establish that a selection was made by a captain.

## Generation, assignment and scoring

When line detail is generated, unresolved choice collections receive untyped child placeholders with stable
matchUpIds, collectionId and collectionPosition. They remain visible as pending choice rubbers; they must not
be classified as singles or doubles by default. The effective collection declaration supplies their choice
context; do not duplicate mutable option lists on every stored child.

An unresolved rubber cannot receive player/pair assignments, scores, results or start-of-play actions.
Preliminary team-roster selection is still possible, but collection assignments must wait for resolution.
Selecting the type atomically updates the encounter's effective collection and all its child matchUpTypes,
without replacing their IDs or schedules. Existing assignment methods then apply normal singles/doubles
cardinality, membership and eligibility checks. A schedule placement lock remains intact.

Changing a previous selection is permitted only while every affected rubber is unplayed, with no winner,
entered score or actual-play evidence, and the parent encounter has no result. Another collection may already
be underway. Existing player/pair assignments require explicit clearing; do not choose a remaining singles
player or manufacture a doubles partner. Clear only the affected collection assignments, preserving rosters,
other collections, shared pair records and assignment metadata. Override inherited lineups locally so they do
not silently restore cleared assignments. Generic in-progress overrides cannot bypass these protections.

Clearing a selection is a distinct, explicit reset: retain the declaration and placeholder IDs, remove the
local concrete selection and child types, and return the collection to unresolved. Apply the same play and
assignment protections. Omitting `matchUpType` from a modification request is not a reset. The mutation/API
shape for this reset remains to be designed before implementation.

## Inheritance, tallying and persistence

- Declaration inheritance follows normal tieFormat resolution: matchUp, structure, drawDefinition, event.
  A concrete choice is local to its TEAM encounter. It does not propagate to subsequent encounters with a
  team's lineup, nor resolve another encounter that shares the template.
- Options survive selection, copy-on-write, aggregation/deduplication, serialization and reload. Choice and
  fixed-type collections are different definitions even if they currently select the same concrete type.
  Comparison must include the declaration, not just the selected matchUpType/count/value.
- Collection count and possible value are known before selection. Selection alone does not change winCriteria,
  result values, standings or the parent score. Pending choice rubbers have no results to tally; an encounter
  can still be clinched by other collections under its normal winCriteria without playing the choice rubber.
- For `scoreSource: REPORTED`, the declaration may describe permitted choices without generating lines.
  An absent reported selection is unknown; never invent one from the aggregate team result.
- Resolution/reselection/reset must preflight the whole operation before writes, preserve state and emit no
  mutation notices on refusal, and announce the final parent/changed children on success. Preserve the
  declaration in audit/persistence records as well as the selected type.
- NOC extension migration must be explicit. Keep the extension until its declaration, selections and unresolved
  cases have been verified against this first-class contract; do not infer a selection from its old DOUBLES
  storage placeholder. New-use validation must refuse unsupported choice declarations until runtime support
  is available. Tolerant historical readers may retain metadata, but may not treat retention as scoring support.

## Implementation acceptance

Before enabling this contract, add the typed collection field and validator, update generation and all
assignment/scoring/start-of-play entry points to refuse unresolved choices, and make resolution/reset atomic.
Audit comparison, copying, centralized aggregation, hierarchy, hydration, query filters and persistence so the
declaration and local selection survive without leaking into other encounters. Coordinate separately with
outcome-pipeline owners before changing any status/outcome path; this specification changes no such code.

Regression coverage must include unresolved generation; both selections and assignment cardinalities;
out-of-options/invalid declarations; unchanged options after reselection/reset; incompatible gender; played
rubber and finished-parent refusal; inherited assignments; schedule locks; shared-format replay identity;
notice correctness; independent encounters and later rounds; and JSON round trips. Score-source and tally
checks must prove that declaring/resolving a choice does not fabricate a result or change its value model.
