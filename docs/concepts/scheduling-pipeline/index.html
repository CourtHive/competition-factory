The scheduling pipeline

Automated scheduling places matchUps in time and on courts. Two pipelines do it and they compose: the timed pipeline (scheduleProfileRounds) gives each matchUp a date and a time; the grid pipeline (scheduleProfileGrid) gives each matchUp a court and an order on a day's court grid. A third, scheduleMatchUps, times an explicit list of matchUps on one date. All three share one core: the dependency graph, the participant reachability model, the timing rules, recovery, daily limits, person requests and the order rule. This page states the rules a second implementation has to reproduce: the inputs, the core, each pipeline's order of decisions, what is written, what is reported, and the guarantees. Where the engine's behaviour is a question rather than a decision it is marked OPEN.

First version, 2026-10-23, written from the source and from the golden corpus (Mentat/planning/SCHEDULING_PIPELINE_VNEXT.md § 6). The corpus pins it with authored scenarios (authored/scheduling/*), a 288-cell scale matrix (pnpm corpus:scheduling-scale), and seven scheduling properties checked on every scheduling call the test suite makes (SCHEDULING_PROPERTIES=<file> pnpm vitest run).

scheduleProfileRounds (timed)          scheduleProfileGrid (grid)            scheduleMatchUps (direct)
  per date, per venue:                   per date:                              one date, listed matchUps:
    profiled rounds → Garman slots         profiled rounds → court-grid rows      Garman slots → place
    slot by slot: gate → place             row by row: rules → place
  writes date, time, venue               writes date, court, courtOrder         writes time (date in it), venue
          └──────────────── shared core: graph · reachability · timing · recovery · limits · requests · played order

1. The inputs​

The scheduling profile lists, per date, the venues in play and, per venue, the rounds to schedule there in order. A round names a tournament, event, draw, structure and round number; it may narrow itself to matchUpIds, or to a roundSegment (segmentNumber of segmentsCount, by round position). A round's own periodLength is not read: the call's sizes every venue's periods. A round robin's rounds are profiled on the round robin's containing structure: they cover every group it contains.

The call takes scheduleDates (the profile's dates to schedule; all of them when absent), clearScheduleDates, courtIds (a court filter), periodLength (minutes per scheduling period; when absent, the smaller of 30 and a venue's T plus recovery, so a short format is not given periods longer than itself) and dryRun. A completed matchUp is never scheduled again, and there is no override (CA 2026-10-23): scheduleCompletedMatchUps is accepted and ignored by the schedulers until the next major. mocksEngine, auto-scheduling a generated tournament, schedules it first and applies its results after, as a real tournament is played.

allowInactiveEventDates (default false), taken by all four schedulers (scheduleProfileRounds, scheduleProfileGrid, proAutoSchedule, scheduleMatchUps): see § 3.10. When it is set, a matchUp is placed on a day its event does not play, and each such placement is warned about.

clearScheduleDates. true clears every scheduled matchUp that is not finished, in every tournament loaded, on every date, profiled or not; a schedule lock survives. An array clears only the matchUps scheduled on the dates it lists. The clear happens before any placement.

What the record supplies: courts and their availability per date (with bookings), the scheduling policy (timing and daily limits) or its default, the tournament's own daily limits, person requests, and every matchUp's existing schedule and lock.

2. Refusals​

  • No valid date remains after narrowing the profile's dates by scheduleDates: NO_VALID_DATES.
  • An empty profile is not refused: the call succeeds and places nothing.
  • Before scheduling, the profile is validated against the tournament. A profile that names a venue, an event or a draw no longer in the record needs repair: the call is refused with SCHEDULING_PROFILE_REPAIR_REQUIRED and writes nothing. Its context offers the repaired profile (repairedSchedulingProfile, without those rounds), with modifications and issues; saving it with setSchedulingProfile is the repair. A round profiled at more than one venue on a date is reported in schedulingProfileIssues.
  • A venue with no courts (none added yet, or all removed) is still a venue (CA 2026-10-23): its rounds are kept and place nothing. The timed pipeline reports their matchUps in noTimeMatchUpIds; the grid does not offer them to the date's other courts and reports them in notScheduledMatchUpIds.
  • Reading the profile never saves a repair: getSchedulingProfile returns the repaired profile with its modifications and leaves the stored one as it is. A query runs on one side only (client or server), so a save there would part the two records. The mutations that remove a venue, event or draw (or a venue's courts) repair the profile as part of their own write.
  • A profile that orders a round before a round it depends on is not refused and not reported by the scheduler (the query getSchedulingProfileIssues reports such rounds, with the matchUps each shouldBeAfter). The gate's dependency rule (§ 4, step 4.3) still places the rounds in dependency order.

OPEN: the remaining parameter refusals (INVALID_VALUES for a malformed courtIds or periodLength) are not yet pinned by the corpus.

3. The shared core​

3.1 The dependency graph​

A matchUp depends on every matchUp upstream of it: through winner links, loser links (consolation and feed-in), qualifying-to-main links (a qualifier position depends on the qualifying round that fills it) and round robin group to playoff links. The relation is transitive: a final depends on every matchUp of its draw that can feed it. AD_HOC rounds and the rounds of a round robin carry no links and impose no order on each other.

3.2 Participant reachability​

For each matchUp, who might play it, across all dates:

  • certain: in the matchUp's sides;
  • potential: can still reach it through a source matchUp not yet decided. A feeder's winner path and loser path are alternatives for its participants, never both;
  • out: lost a completed feeder and has no route to it (a completed feeder sends only its winner on and only its loser to the consolation).

Near potentials are those one matchUp away: the participants of a matchUp's direct, undecided sources.

3.3 Timing​

Each matchUp has an average duration, a recovery time and a type-change recovery time (used when the next matchUp a participant plays is of the other matchUpType). They are resolved from:

  1. minutes the call states (scheduleMatchUps only: averageMatchUpMinutes, recoveryMinutes, recoveryMinutesMap);
  2. otherwise the event's schedule timing, the tournament's scheduling.timing, or the attached scheduling policy, by the matchUp's format and its event's category;
  3. otherwise the default scheduling policy (POLICY_SCHEDULING_DEFAULT): a standard SET3-S:6/TB7 singles averages 90 minutes and recovers 60.

A timed format carries its own average when no rule names it: a whole-match cap (-M:T60) is the average outright; otherwise best-of × set minutes, with the final set's minutes substituted when the code gives them (SET3-S:T15-F:T10 is 40). The floor, when nothing applies, is 90 minutes and no recovery.

scheduleMatchUps with no timing stated anywhere (no call minutes, no policy, no timing on the event or tournament) uses 90 minutes and no recovery, as it always has.

3.4 Recovery​

A participant is free after a matchUp at its start plus its average plus its recovery (or its type-change recovery for a following matchUp of the other type). On one date, a matchUp is placed only where every participant it holds is free: its certain participants, and its near potentials (a participant one matchUp away is booked as a potential; participants further away are not, by design, 2026-10-16). A matchUp already timed on the date holds its participants whether or not the profile is scheduling it, completed or not, for its own minutes. A recorded endTime replaces the average in the free time.

Its own minutes (CA 2026-10-25): a matchUp already timed on the date holds its participants for its own average (from its format) plus its own recovery, once, whatever venues the date profiles; a matchUp the plan places holds them for its round's average, the same figure its recovery is checked with. No figure from its venue or another round lengthens or shortens it (the engine used the greatest average of any venue profiled on the date). A matchUp about to be scheduled raises no not-before time from where it was timed before, which may be another date: the matchUps it feeds wait only for where it is placed now.

Transit between venues (CA 2026-10-25). When a participant's consecutive matchUps on a date are at different venues, the later may not start before the earlier's play ends plus the greater of its recovery (or type-change recovery) and the transit from the earlier's venue to the later's: recovery counts toward transit. Either order is checked: a matchUp placed before another already timed at another venue must also leave the transit before it. Transit holds participants, never courts. The minutes from one venue to another are, in order: the minutes stated for that direction (a pair stated once applies both ways; a one-way value only its way); none when the two venues share a facilityId (one physical place); else the scheduling policy's defaultTimes.transitMinutes (the default policy states 30). With no policy stating it there is no transit, and a matchUp with no venue, or two matchUps at the same venue, take none. Minutes are whole. A record states the pairs once each in scheduling.venueTransits ({ venueIds, minutes, oneWay?, sanctioned? }; a sanctioned value is a floor the minutes never go below), pruned when a venue is deleted; where linked records state one pair differently, the greater applies. The planning view carries this as transit (defaultMinutes, pairs by direction, facilityIds).

3.5 Daily limits​

A participant's day load is every matchUp they are certain to play on the date, plus, per draw, the longest chain of matchUps they can only potentially play there: a run of matchUps each of which can feed the next. Two potential matchUps where neither feeds the other are alternatives (a winner path and a loser path) and do not add (CA, 2026-10-16). Draws add. A matchUp dated on the date counts whether or not it is timed (CA 2026-10-28). A participant's load counts the date's matchUps only: a matchUp on an earlier date, or a completed matchUp with no date, does not count against today, but a participant reachable from an earlier date's feeder counts as a potential of today's matchUps.

The limits are counted by matchUpType and in TOTAL. Each matchUp is checked against the limits of its own tournament (the tournament's scheduling.dailyLimits, else the scheduling policy's defaultDailyLimits; none when neither is set), with the participant's override from the policy's matchUpDailyLimits on top, key by key. A limit of 0 is no limit. A matchUp is over the limit when adding it takes any certain or potential participant's load past a limit.

3.6 Person requests​

A DO_NOT_SCHEDULE request on a date blocks a placement whose projected window (start to start plus the matchUp's own average minutes, its round's; CA 2026-10-25) overlaps the request's window at all. The people checked are the matchUp's individual participants (a pair's two individuals) and, by default, its potential participants'.

3.7 Played order​

A matchUp is never placed at or before a matchUp it depends on, and never after a matchUp that depends on it. This was the played order (CA, 2026-10-22): only a COMPLETED, RETIRED or DEFAULTED matchUp with a score imposed anything. It now binds plans as well as history (CA, 2026-10-30): every matchUp but a BYE orders the matchUps linked to it, walkovers, defaults and double exits included, however far downstream the link runs. The comparison is scheduled against scheduled: an earlier date, or the same date with both times set and at or before. A same-day placement with no time, or a matchUp with no date, proves nothing. The profile scheduler asks it of each slot it offers (§ 4, step 4.2) and then once of its whole plan (§ 4, step 5); every schedule write refuses it with ANACHRONISM, and plannedOrder: false on a write keeps the played order only (see schedule governor: schedule order). The facts it reads (playedOrderFacts) are present for any draw with a placed matchUp, played or not.

3.8 The clock​

Every time the scheduler reads or writes is a wall-clock time at the venue: hours and minutes, with no date and no time zone. The scheduler never converts between zones, and every comparison it makes is on one scheduling day. The canonical form is HH:MM (00–23, 00–59), and every write validates through it and stores it (§ 9.9). The 23:59 cap, which § 9.8 decides to replace, is how the engine reads today. The rules are pinned by the kernel.time.* conformance cases.

  1. A time. A string is a time when it is H:MM or HH:MM, optionally with seconds (HH:MM:SS, a fraction allowed), with hours at most 23, minutes and seconds at most 59; or a 12-hour time, h:mm AM or h:mm PM, hours 1 to 12; or an ISO date-time (rule 3). Nothing before or after it is allowed. Anything else (24:00, 23:60, -1:00, 8:5, 08:00Z, " 8:00", 13:30 PM, 12, noon, the empty string) is not a time.
  2. Tidying. A time is written as HH:MM: 8:00 → 08:00, 08:00:00 → 08:00, 8:30 PM → 20:30, 12:15 AM → 00:15.
  3. A date-time's time. An ISO date-time, zoned or not, contributes the time after its T (2026-03-02T09:15:30.000Z → 09:15); the zone is not applied. A date-time whose date is not a calendar day (rule 7) is not a time. A date and time separated by a space is not read as a time.
  4. Minutes past midnight. A time's hours × 60 + minutes, read from its tidied form, and zero for anything that is not a time.
  5. From minutes. Minutes past midnight are written as HH:MM, the hours wrapping at 24: 1440 is 00:00, 1500 is 01:00.
  6. Within the day. A time plus minutes stays on the scheduling day: a result at or past midnight is 23:59, the end of the day, and a negative one is 00:00. Anything that is not a time counts as 00:00. A recovery that would end after midnight ends at 23:59 on its own day and carries into the next date (§ 9.8): the matchUps timed on the day before, stored there or placed there by the same plan, whose own average and recovery minutes end after midnight book their participants from 00:00 to the minute recovery ends, and the matchUps they feed may not start before it. A matchUp at 23:00 of 90 minutes with a 60-minute recovery holds its participants until 01:30. A court booking that would end past midnight holds its court to 23:59 on its own day and from 00:00 on the next date for the minutes that remain: a matchUp booked at 23:30 for 90 minutes holds the court until 01:00 of the next date (§ 9.8). A booking's end used to wrap, ending at 01:00 of its own day, before it started.
  7. A calendar day. The date part, YYYY-MM-DD, of an ISO date or date-time, and a real day on the (proleptic Gregorian) calendar: 2026-02-30 and 2026-04-31 are not dates, 2028-02-29 is. A date-time is one whose time is a time (rule 1): its date is read as written, the zone not applied. Nothing else is a date: a date and time separated by a space (2026-03-02 09:15), an ISO week date (2026-W10), an ordinal date (2026-061), the basic format (20260302) and a month (2026-03) are not read. Two values are on the same day when both have a date part and the date parts are equal; the time and any zone designator are ignored, and the runtime's zone never enters. A value with no date part is on no day. Every same-day test the scheduler makes (profile dates, stored scheduled dates, court availability dates, person requests) reads it so, and a profile date given as a date-time is stored as its day.
  8. Garman slots are computed in minutes on the scheduling day (§ 4, step 2) and written back as HH:MM.
  9. Not-before times are compared as text. That orders them correctly only because every not-before time the scheduler writes is a tidied HH:MM.

3.9 Court bookings​

A matchUp already timed on the date holds a court; the venue's slots (§ 4 step 2) are computed around what is held. Rules decided 2026-10-25 (debate-ruled, CA-reviewed):

  1. Its own minutes. A timed matchUp holds a court for its own average minutes, resolved as § 3.3 resolves them: the record's timing, else the scheduling policy, else a timed format's own minutes. A timed matchUp with no format takes the event's timing, else the default policy's average (90). It never takes the minutes of the round being placed. Recovery holds participants (§ 3.4), never the court.
  2. A BYE holds nothing. A BYE is never played: whatever schedule it carries, it holds no court, adds nothing to a participant's day (§ 3.5) and raises no not-before time. The lines of a TEAM dual that is a BYE are BYEs too, and no scheduler places them (since 7.23.0; a placement already made is not touched).
  3. No venue. A timed matchUp with no venue is at the tournament's only venue with courts on the date, when there is exactly one; otherwise it holds no court. Its participants are held either way.
  4. A venue but no court. A timed matchUp at a venue with no court holds one court of that venue for its minutes: capacity is the rule, which court is not part of the result. When no court is free for its whole length it still holds one. When no court's free time ends around it (it runs past every court's close), it holds none and is reported in unbookedMatchUpIds[date][venueId] rather than dropped silently.
  5. A booking reads a schedule and a status, nothing else. A malformed matchUp elsewhere in the view (draw positions that are not valid) changes no booking; it used to switch every booking off, so the date read as free. A booking that cannot be read stops the run with its error rather than leaving every court free.

3.10 Event playing days​

An event plays on a calendar day when the day lies in the tournament's range and in the event's range, is one of the tournament's activeDates (else, when it states any, one of its weekdays), and is one of the event's activeDates (else, when it states any, one of its weekdays). The rules, stated once (eventPlaysOnDate):

  1. An empty activeDates or weekdays means unset, never "no days".
  2. activeDates win over weekdays when both are set, at the tournament and at the event.
  3. The weekday is the calendar day's, read in UTC (getUTCDay of the YYYY-MM-DD date): no zone moves it.
  4. An event range that does not intersect the tournament range, or whose bounds are inverted, is ignored: the event's range is then the tournament's (CA 2026-11-01). A missing event bound is the tournament's.
  5. A day that is not a YYYY-MM-DD calendar day does not play (it fails closed).

When a day does not play, the first rule that fails is its reason, in this order: INVALID_DATE, OUTSIDE_TOURNAMENT_DATES, OUTSIDE_EVENT_DATES, TOURNAMENT_INACTIVE_DATE, TOURNAMENT_WEEKDAY, EVENT_INACTIVE_DATE, EVENT_WEEKDAY.

The automated schedulers skip per event (CA 2026-11-01): a matchUp is never placed on a day its own event does not play, whatever other events play that day. Each scheduler reports what it skipped in eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds, { [date]: [{ matchUpId, eventId, reason }] }, present only when it skipped something. With allowInactiveEventDates nothing is skipped: the matchUp is placed and the result carries one EVENT_DATE_INACTIVE warning per such placement in warnings ({ code, message, matchUpId, eventId, scheduledDate, reason }). A director's manual placement on such a day is written and warned about the same way, never refused; that is not a scheduler's decision and is outside this page.

4. The timed pipeline: scheduleProfileRounds​

The call runs in three stages: a view, everything the decisions below read, resolved once from the records; a plan, made from the view alone; and the writes. The view is plain data (the hydrated matchUps, the dependency graph, reachability, the profile resolved to rounds and minutes, each date's available courts, every event's timing, the minutes of matchUps already timed on a planned date, transit between venues, and the stored facts of every draw anyone has played in), so a plan can be reproduced from a view without the records: a view written as JSON and read back plans the same.

Dates are taken in order. On each date:

  1. What is already there. The matchUps already timed on the date are booked first, with their own minutes, so they hold their participants for recovery and count toward daily loads. A BYE's schedule is never cleared here: an operator's placement on a seat that became a BYE is preserved.

  2. Per venue, the profiled rounds are taken in sortOrder (then profile order) and resolved to their matchUps in matchUp order, skipping BYEs, completed matchUps, and matchUps already timed. A matchUp dated on the date without a time is not already timed: it is timed like any other, one already on the court grid (a court or a court order) included, and the conflicts its time makes there are reported (§ 7). It is never dropped (CA 2026-10-28). A matchUp belongs to the first venue that profiles it on the date. A round whose event does not play on the date (§ 3.10) is skipped there: it is resolved to no matchUps and no minutes, so it takes no slot and does not count toward the venue's T; its schedulable matchUps not yet timed are reported in eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds and also in noTimeMatchUpIds (unless the call sets allowInactiveEventDates, when the round is resolved like any other). The skip is decided in the view (§ 4, kernel.scheduling.view@1, whose options include allowInactiveEventDates); the plan reads the view alone and is unchanged (kernel.scheduling.plan@2). A roundSegment applies only when the round has a power-of-two count of matchUps, the segment count is a power of two smaller than it, and the round lists no matchUpIds. Each round's matchUps take the minutes of the round's first matchUp's format (OPEN, C7: a round of mixed formats is timed by its first). A round none of whose matchUps has a format cannot be timed: nothing of it is placed, and its matchUps not yet timed are reported in noFormatMatchUpIds. The venue's slots are its Garman times: with C courts available, average minutes T and period length L, the matchUps expected by period P are TM = ((L × C) / T) × (P − 1) + C, rounded to the nearest match, and a period's slots are the difference from the period before, computed over each court's availability and bookings. Periods number from 0 at the venue's opening, which takes C; period 1 adds none. This is Brian Garman's own numbering and rounding ("The Revised Garman System", 2009-10: 10 courts of 90-minute matches total 10, 10, 13, 17, 20, … 77, 80 over 23 half-hour periods). T is the greatest average among the venue's rounds on the date (the rounds form a single group), and L is the call's periodLength (§ 1). Further (decided 2026-10-25):

    • C at a period is the mean of the courts available at the starts of periods 1..P (period 0 takes the courts available at the opening), so a court that opens later adds slots gradually rather than as though it had been open all day; a court is available at a period's start when it is free for T minutes from it;
    • period 0 starts at the earliest time, on or after the start of the day, when some court has a free stretch of at least T: bookings before it (practice, maintenance, matchUps already timed) delay the opening;
    • a period adds nothing when no court is available; after a period with none it adds the courts newly available (an opening, or a reopening after a closure); otherwise it adds the change in TM;
    • the day runs from the courts' earliest opening to their latest closing on the date, unless the call bounds it.

  3. Slot by slot. Each slot is offered to the venue's remaining matchUps in profile order; the first that passes the gate is placed there. A venue's pass places at most as many matchUps as it has courts available on the date (within courtIds; CA 2026-10-25). The engine placed one more, and counted every court of the venue; within one venue this changes nothing, since a slot no matchUp takes goes to the back of the venue's slots, but across venues sharing participants the turns decide which venue reaches a participant first. A slot no matchUp takes is retried in a later pass, up to 10 attempts. Venues alternate pass by pass until every venue is complete or the passes run out (bounded by the day's slots).

  4. The gate, in order; the first failure defers the matchUp for this slot, recorded under its reason:

    1. a participant would go over a daily limit (§ 3.5): overLimitMatchUpIds. A participant's day only gains matchUps as slots are filled, so the matchUp stays over the limit for the rest of the date: it leaves the date's list and is reported once (one overLimitMatchUpDetails entry, at the first slot it was offered), not under noTimeMatchUpIds;
    2. the slot is at or before a played matchUp it depends on, or at or after a played matchUp that depends on it (§ 3.7): playedOrderDeferredMatchUpIds, with the first such slot and the played matchUps that refused it. The slot stays free for the next matchUp, and a matchUp still unplaced at the end of the date is reported under this reason alone;
    3. a matchUp it feeds is already placed earlier than this one would be free: not placed here;
    4. a matchUp it depends on, among the date's matchUps, is not yet placed: dependencyDeferredMatchUpIds;
    5. a participant is not yet free (§ 3.4), or the matchUp's not-before time (its sources' free time) is later: recoveryTimeDeferredMatchUpIds;
    6. a person request blocks it (§ 3.6): requestConflicts.

    A matchUp that passes is booked: its participants' loads and free times are updated for the rest of the date.

  5. The guard, then the write. Once every date is planned, the whole plan is judged against the played order (§ 3.7) together, on the state it leaves, before anything is written. A plan the gate made passes it. One that does not is refused whole with ANACHRONISM, every offending placement in context.refusals, and nothing is written: the guard is a planner defect's last stop, not a reason a TD meets. Then each placement is given its date, its time (HH:MM) and its venue. A date the write refuses (a profile date outside the tournament's dates, which setSchedulingProfile does not check) writes nothing for that placement, neither time nor venue: the matchUp is reported in that date's noTimeMatchUpIds, not in its scheduledMatchUpIds. A dry run does not write, so it does not see that refusal and reports the placement as planned.

Dates named in clearScheduleDates are planned on a working copy of the records cleared first, so the plan sees them empty and a refusal leaves them as they were; they are cleared on the records only when the plan is written. What remains unplaced on a date is reported in noTimeMatchUpIds for that date, beside whatever reasons it collected. A dry run plans exactly as a run does (clearing on the copy included), so it reports what the run would write, and writes nothing. The call records an audit of what it did on the tournament (with eventDateSkippedMatchUpIdsCount when it skipped a matchUp for its event's dates; absent otherwise). When it times a matchUp already on a date's court grid, the grid's conflicts that name it are reported per date in proConflicts (a dry run reports none).

5. The grid pipeline: scheduleProfileGrid​

Per date, the profiled rounds of every venue are resolved to their matchUps (a round on a round robin covers its groups), skipping BYEs, completed matchUps and matchUps already on a court or a court order. A matchUp whose event does not play on the date (§ 3.10) is not offered to the grid: it is reported in eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds and also in notScheduledMatchUpIds. proAutoSchedule, which plans one date's grid from the matchUps it is given, skips the same way, before its view is built, and reports a skipped matchUp both in notScheduled and in eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds; the grid's date view (kernel.scheduling.grid@1) never holds one. The date's grid is its courts by rows; matchUps already timed are walked earliest first, so they land on earlier rows.

Each date is planned from its own view, read from the records as the dates before it left them, so a placement on an earlier date is seen by a later one: the date's grid (its courts by rows, the cells already taken and the free cells on the call's courts), the matchUps to place, the dependency graph and the reachability over them and the matchUps already on the grid, the courts' closures, and the limits. The date's plan reads nothing else (kernel.scheduling.grid@1): each placement is a matchUp and the cell it takes (its court, venue and court order).

Row by row, each remaining matchUp is placed on the row's first open court unless:

  • a matchUp it depends on, or one that depends on it, is already on the row;
  • a matchUp it depends on is still unplaced;
  • a matchUp that depends on it is on the previous row;
  • a participant who can play it is already on the row (unless the two are alternatives for them);
  • a participant would go over a daily limit (§ 3.5): reported over the limit. The grid applies the limits the timed pipeline resolves (CA 2026-10-23); a call given matchUpDailyLimits applies those instead, and one given {} opts out: the matchUp is placed and reported in dailyLimitWarningMatchUpIds;
  • every open court is closed at its time (a time-window court booking).

A matchUp not placed on a row is offered to the next. The placements are written in one bulk write (date, court and court order; refused whole if it breaks the played order) and the call reports, per date, scheduledMatchUpIds (what the write wrote), notScheduledMatchUpIds (everything else, including a matchUp the write skipped as completed, locked or not found) and overLimitMatchUpIds. A date the grid skips (no court in the courtIds filter, or a refused write) reports all its matchUps as not scheduled.

The two pipelines compose. Run timed then grid, a matchUp gets a date and time, then a court and an order on that date, earlier times on earlier rows.

6. The direct scheduler: scheduleMatchUps​

scheduleMatchUps times the listed matchUps, in the order listed, on one scheduleDate, at the venues given (all when absent), within startTime and endTime when given. It shares the core (§ 3) and the slot arithmetic (§ 4, step 2); it differs from the timed pipeline in what it is given (a list, not a profile), in how its minutes are resolved, and in running one pass over one pool of slots rather than venue by venue.

Refusals. No matchUpIds (or not a list): MISSING_MATCHUP_IDS; no records: MISSING_TOURNAMENT_RECORDS. A scheduleDate that is not a date: INVALID_DATE. An averageMatchUpMinutes, recoveryMinutes or periodLength that is not a number: INVALID_VALUES.

  1. The matchUps. The listed matchUps, in the order listed, that are not already timed on the date at the venues of rule 3, have no winner, are not BYE, DEFAULTED, COMPLETED, ABANDONED, RETIRED or WALKOVER, and have no BYE side (a line of a BYE dual). A listed id not found is dropped. A listed matchUp dated on the date without a time is not already there: it is timed, one already on the court grid included, and counts toward its participants' loads when it is taken (CA 2026-10-28). Only a matchUp timed on the date holds a court (rule 3). A listed matchUp dated on another day is scheduled on that day: the call never moves it to its own date, and reports it in noTimeMatchUpIds (CA 2026-10-28). No matchUp changes day through scheduleMatchUps. A listed matchUp whose event does not play on scheduleDate (§ 3.10) is not timed: it is reported in eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds and also in noTimeMatchUpIds, unless the call sets allowInactiveEventDates.
  2. The minutes (§ 3.3, rule 1, and Q1, CA 2026-10-22). Minutes the call states win: averageMatchUpMinutes (default 90), recoveryMinutes (default 0) and recoveryMinutesMap (a recovery per matchUpId). When the call states none, and the tournament or a matchUp's event states timing (an attached scheduling policy, scheduling.timing, or the timing extension), each such matchUp's recovery is its own format's and event's, and the average is the greatest average among them; the matchUps already timed on the date count. When nothing states timing: 90 minutes and no recovery.
  3. The slots (§ 4, step 2), over every court of the venues (venueIds, else every venue) available on the date, from startTime and to endTime when given, else from the earliest opening to the latest closing of those courts, with T the average of rule 2 and L the call's periodLength, 30 when absent. The courts are held by every matchUp of the venues timed on the date, completed or not, each for its own average (§ 3.9 rule 1), and from 00:00 by a matchUp of the day before whose own minutes run past midnight (§ 3.8 rule 6). The slots form one pool, in time order, shared by every venue.
  4. What is already there. Each matchUp timed on the date counts toward its participants' daily loads, and books its participants (§ 3.4) from its time for the average of rule 2 and its recovery. Every matchUp it feeds may not start before its participants are free (its not-before time).
  5. Daily limits first. The matchUps are taken in order; one that would take a participant over a limit (§ 3.5, the limits of the matchUp's own tournament and the policy's per-participant ones, with the call's matchUpDailyLimits as the defaults where a tournament states none; until 2026-10-27 the call's alone) is reported in overLimitMatchUpIds and leaves the list; each other is counted toward its participants' daily loads. Limits are judged once, before any slot, on the list as a whole; a matchUp left unplaced after rule 6 is taken off the loads again.
  6. Slot by slot. Each slot in turn is offered to the remaining matchUps in order; the first that passes the gate is placed there, and booked (§ 3.4). A slot no matchUp takes is skipped (skippedScheduleTimes), and is not offered again; the pass ends when the slots or the matchUps run out. The gate, in order:
    1. a matchUp it depends on, among the listed matchUps and those timed on the date (allDateMatchUpIds when the call states them), is not yet placed: deferred (dependencyDeferred);
    2. a participant is not free in the matchUp's window, or its not-before time is later (§ 3.4, either order: the window is its own average and recovery minutes from the slot, so a placement may not run into a participant's later matchUp on the date; a participant held by a potential booking in another draw counts; no transit applies, as no venue is known yet; until 2026-10-27 the window was the slot's start alone): deferred (recoveryTimeDeferred);
    3. a person request blocks it (§ 3.6; potential participants checked unless checkPotentialRequestConflicts is false): not placed here (requestConflicts).
  7. The write. Each placement, tournament by tournament and draw by draw in the order its matchUps were first listed, is judged against the played order (§ 3.7) and, unless refused, written as a time with the date in it (YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM, stored as its date and HH:MM, § 3.8), then its venue when the call names one venue or the records hold only one. A refused placement is reported in noTimeMatchUpIds and gets no venue. A dry run writes nothing and reports what it would place.

What remains unplaced is reported in noTimeMatchUpIds, with what it collected on the way. The call also returns remainingScheduleTimes (the slots no matchUp took, for a caller that schedules in batches and passes them back as remainingScheduleTimes), the participant profiles it built, the not-before times, the overnight rest warnings (§ 9.8), and proConflicts: the court grid's conflicts (proConflicts) that name a matchUp it timed which was already on the grid, such as a time against its court order. A dry run reports none.

OPEN: rule 5 counts every listed matchUp toward its participants' loads before any is placed, and a matchUp left unplaced is uncounted after the pass; a matchUp placed late in the list is therefore judged against loads that include matchUps not yet placed.

7. What is reported​

Per date, every scheduling call reports what it placed (scheduledMatchUpIds) and, per reason, what it left unplaced. The corpus records this report as the scheduling step's value: matchUpIds only, sorted, per date and reason, without the attempts the engine made on the way (those trace one implementation's loop, not a rule). eventDateSkippedMatchUpIds is one of these reasons (its entries' matchUpIds).

7.1 The plan​

The timed pipeline's plan (§ 4) is keyed by canonical date (§ 3.8 rule 7), each date planned once. Its normative part, what kernel.scheduling.plan@2 compares (CA 2026-10-25):

  • placements[date][venueId]: { matchUpId, scheduleTime } in write order (venue, tournament, draw, profile order); scheduledMatchUpIds[date]: the same ids, in the same order;
  • matchUpScheduleTimes[matchUpId]: the time of every matchUp timed on a planned date, stored or placed;
  • unplaced[date][venueId]: the profiled matchUps no slot took; unbookedMatchUpIds[date][venueId]: matchUps timed at a venue with no court that no court could hold (§ 3.9 rule 4); noTimeMatchUpIds[date]: those, each venue's in turn; noFormatMatchUpIds[date]: matchUps of profiled rounds with no format;
  • why a matchUp was refused, each refused matchUp's first refusal with its facts: recoveryTimeDeferredMatchUpIds[date][matchUpId] (scheduleTime, blockingParticipantIds, notBeforeTime), dependencyDeferredMatchUpIds[date][matchUpId] (scheduleTime, remainingDependencies), playedOrderDeferredMatchUpIds[date][matchUpId] (scheduleTime, blockingMatchUps), overLimitMatchUpIds[date] with overLimitMatchUpDetails[date] (the first attempt per matchUp), and requestConflicts[date].

Every planned date has every key, empty when nothing applies. The rest is informative: every later refusal of the same matchUp, skippedScheduleTimes (the slots no matchUp took and how often each was offered) and scheduleTimesRemaining (the slots left over). They trace how one implementation's slot loop ran, depend on its retry bound, and are not compared; kernel.scheduling.plan@1, which compared them too, is superseded.

8. Guarantees​

These are checked on every scheduling call the corpus records and the suite makes (corpus/INVARIANTS.md):

propertystatement
SCHEDULE_NO_BYE_PLACEDa call never places a BYE
SCHEDULE_SOURCES_FIRSTa placed matchUp starts no earlier than every timed matchUp upstream of it is free
SCHEDULE_RECOVERY_RESPECTEDon a date, the later of two matchUps a participant is certain to play starts once the earlier is free
SCHEDULE_DAILY_LIMITS_RESPECTEDa placement never takes a certain participant over a daily limit
SCHEDULE_ACCOUNTEDevery schedulable profiled matchUp left unplaced is reported under a reason
SCHEDULE_REPORT_TRUEthe reported scheduledMatchUpIds are exactly what was placed
SCHEDULE_RERUN_STABLEthe same call made again places nothing

They are stated over certain participants; potential participants are pinned by the authored scenarios. They apply to the calls that decide placements; the bulk and reschedule mutations write the times their caller states.

9. Open questions​

  1. The grid reads daily limits only when the call passes matchUpDailyLimits. Decided (CA 2026-10-23): it resolves them as the timed pipeline does and enforces them; {} opts out and warns (§ 5). SCHEDULE_DAILY_LIMITS_RESPECTED counts court-grid placements too.
  2. The grid places by row order, not time, for matchUps with no time: SCHEDULE_SOURCES_FIRST and recovery are asked of timed placements, so a grid run's ordering is pinned only by its row rules. Should the corpus state a row-order property?
  3. Recovery books potentials one matchUp away only (by design, 2026-10-16): a participant two rounds away is not held. Daily limits count every potential. Is the asymmetry the rule to keep in vNext?
  4. The parameter refusals of § 2 other than NO_VALID_DATES are not yet pinned by corpus scenarios (authored/scheduling/profile-no-valid-dates, empty-profile-places-nothing and profile-out-of-order pin the rest of § 2).
  5. C7: a round of mixed formats is timed by its first matchUp's format (§ 4, step 2). Should each matchUp take its own format's minutes?
  6. The first Garman period is counted twice. Decided (CA 2026-10-24, after research and a debate): the period numbering was Garman's own, period 0 at the opening taking C and period 1 adding none, as every one of his published tables does. The defect was truncation: the tables round each total to the nearest match, and the slots truncated it, leaving a period short whenever the fraction passed one half. They now round (§ 4, step 2), so one court of 90-minute matchUps in 30-minute periods takes its second matchUp at 11:30, when the first is expected to end. Pinned by scheduling.plan.garman-one-court.v1 (kernel cases) and the published 10-court column in garman.test.ts.
  7. Request conflicts are reported beside the dates. Decided (2026-10-24, by two opposed debate agents at CA's request; pending CA review): conflicts sit under their date, { [date]: [{ request, scheduleTimes, matchUpIds }] }, one entry per request a placement broke. Every reader already assumed date keys (the auto-scheduling audit's count, TMX's results drawer, the corpus report) and misread the mixed shape. Each person request also gets its own id: a time code had given every request added in one call the same one. Pinned by scheduling.plan.person-request-on-a-placed-side.v1.
  8. Crossing midnight. DECIDED (CA 2026-10-25): crossed only when necessary, backwards compatible. Built: the date-time arithmetic (kernel.civil.addMinutes@1); court bookings carried into the next date (kernel.time.endOnTheDay@1), in every scheduler that builds bookings; and a participant's recovery carried into the next date by the profile planner (§ 3.8, rule 6). No corpus scenario moved. The policy's overnight rest (overnightTimes, category-dependent: 12 hours for JUNIOR in the default policy, none otherwise) is warned, not held (CA 2026-10-25: a guideline a player may waive). No scheduler moves a placement for it (scheduling.plan.overnight-rest-is-warned-not-held.v1); every scheduler (the profile planner, scheduleMatchUps, the grid and the pro scheduler) returns overnightRestWarnings, a list per date of the participant, the matchUp and its time, the previous matchUp and its end, and the rest found and required, for each date it placed and the date after, a dry run included. The rest is counted from the previous day's last end of play: a recorded endTime (an endTime before its start ended after midnight), else the start plus the average minutes. A matchUp of the day before still undecided warns for the matchUp its winner or loser goes to, with no participantId: whoever advances. Only a placement with a time is measured; a court order alone has none. The category is the event's stated categoryType, else JUNIOR when its ageCategoryCode is an under-age code of 18 or less (U14, 16U): the same reading every timing lookup now makes, so a U-coded event also takes the JUNIOR recovery times. Not yet built: a stored endDate after midnight, which bookings do not read (they take a matchUp's average minutes), and either carry in the direct scheduler. The rule:
    • A date with no time is unchanged. scheduledDate alone remains a complete schedule ("on that day, time to be set"); with no time there is nothing to add minutes to, so no booking and no recovery follow from it.
    • scheduledTime is unchanged. It is the start, HH:MM on the venue's wall clock, on scheduledDate; a start never crosses midnight. A legacy scheduledTime holding an ISO date-time (written when the matchUp had no scheduledDate) is read as that date and time, as today.
    • Date-time arithmetic runs only when a time is present and minutes are added (an end, a recovery, a booking): a calendar date-time, a date and a wall-clock time with no zone on the proleptic Gregorian calendar, in whole minutes. A result before midnight is HH:MM on the same date, exactly as today; only a result past midnight is on the next date (2026-03-02 23:30 plus 90 minutes is 2026-03-03 01:00), and it is stored only where an end is stored already, as endDate.
    • It is UTC-instant arithmetic (UTC has no daylight saving), so it needs no date library: a date-time can be computed as a UTC instant, or as a day count since 1970-01-01 × 1440 plus minutes past midnight. A daylight-saving change inside a matchUp is ignored, by definition. Zones apply only at the edges (now, an embargo, display), always by an explicit IANA zone on the record, never the host's.
    • A booking or a recovery that ends past midnight carries into the next date; overnight rest is counted from the end of play and warned, never held.
    • Nothing carries from a planned date whose schedule the call clears: what was stored there is gone (the court bookings now read this as the recovery carry already did, 2026-10-25).
    • The consequence: a schedule that never crosses midnight is placed exactly as today; only the placements the 23:59 cap and the wrapping booking end decided move.
    • A start after midnight is on the next date (CA 2026-10-25: "if it starts past midnight it is technically scheduled for the next day"): a 00:30 start has the next scheduledDate. There is no separate session day.
  9. The clock reads more than HH:MM (§ 3.8). DECIDED (CA 2026-10-25): invalid forms are refused. Built.
    • At the write, a date must be a real calendar date, YYYY-MM-DD (2026-02-30 is refused), and a time must convert unambiguously to HH:MM (00–23, 00–59), which is what is stored: 8:00, 08:00:00, an ISO date-time and a 12-hour time (8:30 PM is stored as 20:30) are accepted; 23:60, 24:00, -1:00, 08:00Z and " 8:00" are refused. The writes: a matchUp's scheduled time and date and its schedule items; court and venue availability, bookings and a venue's default times; person requests (one request with a date or a time that is not one refuses the call); tournament and event dates, which are checked against the calendar and stored as sent. A scheduled time given as a date-time stores its time, and its date when the matchUp has none; one naming another day than the matchUp's scheduledDate is refused (INVALID_DATE), never stored against it (CA 2026-10-28). A scheduledDate given beside it in a schedule write is the matchUp's date, and the time is written against it.
    • At the read, stored records keep working: the same unambiguous forms are normalised, and anything else reads as no time, as a value that is not a time did before. Production holds no such value (below), so the audit that would list them is not built.
    • Measured 2026-10-25 (read-only, every tournament record in production): scheduled times 43,527, all canonical; court availability times 8,682 and dates 4,473, all canonical; scheduled dates 66,580 canonical and 154 empty strings (a cleared date, which already reads as no date); no date out of the calendar. So the refusals change no stored schedule, and the empty-string date is the only legacy form the read has to keep reading as absent. The match clock's times (start, stop, resume, end) are a different kind of value, instants: 10,395 are ISO instants (…T…Z) and 746 bare HH:MM; they are outside this rule.
