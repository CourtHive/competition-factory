What hydration derives

A stored draw holds matchUps as written: their positions, schedules, scores and the ids of the matchUps they feed. Hydration turns them into matchUps in context: each side's participant, the round's context, the schedule with its recovery time, the matchUps it feeds, and the participants who may yet arrive. This page states what hydration derives, and from what, precisely enough for a second implementation to reproduce it from a stored draw. Its scope is the fields the scheduling pipeline reads (the scheduling pipeline); the planning view serializes exactly these for kernel.scheduling.plan@2, and kernel.scheduling.view@1 grades the view built from the records. The rulings cited are CA's of 2026-10-26.

1. The merge​

Each matchUp in context is built in three layers, a later layer winning:

  1. Context inherited from the tournament, the event and the draw (tournamentId, eventId, the event's matchUpFormat, its category, …).
  2. Derived values: the format and type, the round's context, the schedule, the draw positions.
  3. The stored matchUp itself: any attribute stored on the matchUp overrides the derived one.

Absent, null and empty values never override. Three derived values win over what is stored:

  • schedule is rebuilt (section 4); of the stored schedule only calledAt and scoredTime are kept.
  • sides are rebuilt after the merge (section 3); a stored side is spread under the derived one.
  • A line of a TEAM dual that is a BYE is a BYE, whatever the line stores (ruling OPEN-1). The dual carries the BYE; its lines stay stored TO_BE_PLAYED.

2. Identity, format and type​

fieldrule
tournamentId, eventId, drawIdfrom the containers
structureIdthe structure holding the matchUp; in a round robin, the GROUP's
stage, stageSequencethe top-level structure's (a round robin's container)
roundNumber, roundPositionstored; a TEAM line takes its dual's roundNumber and has no roundPosition
matchUpFormatthe matchUp's own, else the structure's, the draw's, the event's. A TEAM line: its collection's format, else the same chain
matchUpTypethe matchUp's, the collection's, the structure's, the draw's, else the event's type (not TEAM); else inferred from the first side's participant
matchUpStatus, winningSide, scorestored (a BYE dual's lines: section 1)

A TEAM line resolves its tieFormat through its dual (ruling OPEN-4): the line's own, else the dual's, which falls back to the structure, the draw and the event. A tieFormatId is resolved before any inline default: the referenced tieFormat in the draw's tieFormats, then the event's, then the draw's own tieFormat, then the event's.

3. Sides​

Draw positions are read, never derived. A matchUp's drawPositions are those it stores (a TEAM line: its dual's). A later round holds a position only once a result, a BYE or an exit advanced it.

Side order. Two positions: ascending, side 1 the lower. One position: in a feed round, side 2 when the position advanced from the round before, side 1 when it was fed; otherwise the side whose feeding matchUp it came from (the round's paired positions). None: both sides empty.

Side content. A side's participantId is the structure's position assignment for its draw position (a round robin: the groups' assignments together); an assignment marked bye makes a BYE side. A TEAM line's side is the player (SINGLES) or the PAIR participant of the two players (DOUBLES) the team's lineUp assigns to the line's collection position; the lineUp is the dual side's, else the draw's lineUps by the team's participantId (ruling OPEN-12). A DOUBLES line's side has a participantId only when that PAIR exists: hydration never creates participants (ruling OPEN-6).

sides[].participant and its person.personId come from the tournament's participants, through any participant privacy policy: data outside the draw.

The round profile (round counts and paired positions, roundFactor, feedRound) of a round robin is its container's, computed over all its groups. Generated round robins have groups of equal size (BYE positions pad them), so it equals each group's; an imported draw with unequal groups differs only in the display-only roundFactor and feedRound (ruling OPEN-13).

4. Schedule​

Each attribute is read from the first-class schedule when present, else from the latest timeItem of its type, the last of those stamped alike: scheduledDate (else the date of a date-time scheduledTime), scheduledTime, venueId, courtId, and endTime (ruling OPEN-9).

Recovery (when asked for, as the scheduling view asks): with a scheduledTime and a known type, the matchUp's average and recovery minutes for its format and category, from the scheduling policy of the draw, else the event, else the tournament, else the default policy (ruling OPEN-11), and the tournament's and event's timing settings.

  • timeAfterRecovery is HH:MM: the end of play plus the recovery minutes, or the start plus the average and recovery minutes. dateAfterRecovery is the day it falls on: the matchUp's date, or the next day past midnight (ruling OPEN-8). An end of play recorded as a time earlier than the start was after midnight. An unparseable time gives neither.
  • typeChangeTimeAfterRecovery and typeChangeDateAfterRecovery likewise, with the singles-to-doubles recovery.
  • The arithmetic is on minutes past midnight: no clock, no time zone (ruling OPEN-7).

A format's minutes are resolved block by block, the first block holding an entry for the category deciding: the event's timing by format, the tournament's, the policy's by format, the policy's defaultTimes, and finally a default average of 90 minutes and recovery of 0. An entry applies when it names no category, or names the event's category name or its timing category type; within a block, entries naming more categories are asked first. With no scheduling policy attached, the default scheduling policy is the policy. A timed format's own length (a whole-match cap, else the set minutes times the sets, the final set's own minutes for the last) replaces both the flat default average and the policy's defaultTimes average; a policy entry naming the format still wins. A recovery entry applies when its average range holds the matchUp's average, or when it names the format. Of an entry, the event type's minutes, else its default; the singles-to-doubles change (SINGLES_DOUBLES for a singles matchUp, DOUBLES_SINGLES for a doubles one), else the recovery.

5. The graph​

Hydration adds, for every matchUp of an elimination structure, the matchUp its winner goes to and the matchUp its loser goes to; targets never cross draws.

  • Stored ids first. A matchUp storing its edges (winnerMatchUpId, and loserMatchUpId wherever a loser link exists) uses them: every generated draw stores them. A matchUp storing only one keeps it and derives the other from the links (ruling OPEN-10).
  • Derived from the links of the source round: the target round's matchUp at the position the link's feed profile gives; with no winner link, the next round of the same structure.
  • winnerTo and loserTo describe the target (id, structure, round, position, schedule). loserTo skips a BYE: a loser whose target is a BYE goes on to the next matchUp to be played, and loserTo names that one.
  • sidesTo (round robin groups and other win-ratio structures, which have no winnerTo/loserTo): for each of the matchUp's positions, the next round's matchUp holding it.
  • A TEAM line has no edges: the graph lives on its dual, reached through matchUpTieId (ruling OPEN-5).
  • A target whose position the target structure marks disableLinks is no target: nothing goes there.

schedule.scheduleConflict is set on a target scheduled before the source's recovery ends, comparing the date and the time where both dates are known (ruling OPEN-8).

6. Potential participants​

The participants who may arrive at a matchUp from an undecided matchUp feeding it, one round back:

  • A source contributes when it has a draw position and a side with a participant, is not a BYE, and is not decided.
  • To its winner target, its participants, unless one of them already stands there; to its loser target, likewise.
  • The loser target is the matchUp the loser actually reaches (the one loserTo names); no BYE holds potentials (ruling OPEN-3).
  • In a first-match-loser consolation, a loser target receiving fewer than two participants also receives { bye: true, tbd: true }: an exit could yet send someone. The marker is the loser target's only (ruling OPEN-2).
  • One group per contributing source, in source order. A TEAM line, and a win-ratio structure's matchUps, have none.

7. Order and state​

The matchUps come in the order of the records, each record's events in order, each event's draws in order, then the record's matchUps outside any draw. The record set is keyed by tournamentId, so it is walked as its keys are: ids that are array indices first, ascending, then the others in the order they were added.

Within a draw, by state, each state's matchUps in structure order:

  1. Abandoned: status ABANDONED.
  2. Completed: a completed status (CANCELLED, COMPLETED, DEAD_RUBBER, DEFAULTED, DOUBLE_DEFAULT, DOUBLE_WALKOVER, RETIRED, WALKOVER) or a winning side; unless it is a BYE matchUp, which comes last.
  3. Upcoming: an upcoming status (CHALLENGED, IN_PROGRESS, INCOMPLETE, SUSPENDED, TO_BE_PLAYED) with both positions filled; a TEAM line with both sides assigned.
  4. Pending: the rest.
  5. BYE: a matchUp holding a position assigned a BYE (a TEAM line never, by position).

Structure order: by stage (qualifying, main, play-off and consolation, voluntary consolation), then round target, then the most positions assigned, then stage sequence, then the least finishing positions. Within a structure the stored order; a round robin's groups in order; a structure's TEAM lines after all its matchUps, dual by dual.

The graph and the potentials are added in this order, so a target's potential participants are grouped in the order their sources come here.
