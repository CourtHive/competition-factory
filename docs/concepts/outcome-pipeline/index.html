The outcome pipeline

Every result a matchUp receives travels one path: setMatchUpStatus validates and orchestrates, setMatchUpState decides, attemptToSetMatchUpStatus routes, and modifyMatchUpScore is the one place matchUp.score and matchUp.matchUpStatus are written. What that write causes, direction of the winner and loser and propagation of an exit, is the rest of the pipeline. This page states the rules a second implementation has to reproduce: the inputs, every refusal by its error code, the routes, what a write does, the side effects in order, and the guarantees. Where the engine's behaviour is a question rather than a decision it is marked OPEN.

First version, 2026-10-01, written from the source and from the golden corpus's measurements. The pipeline is 6,548 lines across twelve files; 281 test files touch it; the corpus records 50,559 setMatchUpStatus steps and has observed eighteen distinct refusal codes from it.

1. The inputs​

An outcome carries at most score, matchUpStatus, winningSide and matchUpStatusCodes, and optionally a matchUpFormat. The call also takes matchUpId, a draw (drawId or drawDefinition), a schedule to apply once accepted, notes, and the flags below.

Score strings are derived, never accepted. When score.sets is present, empty sets are dropped and scoreStringSide1 / scoreStringSide2 are regenerated from the sets under the matchUp's effective format. A caller's strings are discarded. score.sets is the single source of truth.

Three flags, one precedence rule: the policy governs, both ways (CA, 2026-10-01).

flagprecedenceabsent both
allowChangePropagationpolicy ?? param ?? undefinednot allowed
propagateExitStatuspolicy ?? param ?? undefinedno
propagateRetirementAsExitpolicy ?? param ?? falseno

An applied scoring policy that speaks on a flag, true or false, wins over anything on the call; the call decides only where the policy is silent. So under a policy that propagates retirement as an exit, a tournament director passing false is overruled, and under a policy that forbids it, a director passing true is overruled too: "if a governance policy is someone who retires can no longer continue playing, a tournament director under that policy shouldn't be able to allow a participant to continue in the draw." POLICY_SCORING_DEFAULT is silent on all three, so a provider that attaches it leaves the decision to the call. Until 2026-10-01 the call won on propagateRetirementAsExit and a truthy call won on the other two (||), so a caller could override its federation's rule; the three authored scenarios authored/outcome-pipeline/flags-* pin the new contract from both directions.

A matchUpFormat on the call is validated before anything runs and persisted only once the outcome is accepted. It used to be written first, so a refused outcome left a new format behind.

2. The refusals, in the order they are checked​

A refused call returns one ErrorType with a code and changes nothing (§ 6); its context, when present, is an object, and a sentence for a person goes in info (two row-9 refusals returned the sentence AS context until the authored scenarios refused to record them, 2026-10-01). The checks run in this order; the first that fails is the answer. Two corrections from S2a (2026-10-01), measured by running v2 differentially against v1: the TEAM clause of row 5 (AWAITING_RESULT) is asked after row 10, and the participants clause of row 6 (§ 2.1) after row 11; and row 9's second condition judges the stored score under the stored format while its third judges the incoming score under the format the call would apply, so a call that carries a new matchUpFormat can be refused under the old one. The checks v1's writers make before their first write, after the dispatch (rows 13 to 15), are asked there (S7, refuseRoute): row 24's removal clause, row 28 and row 6's fallthrough clause.

2.1 Who may receive a directing outcome​

A directing outcome (a winner, or COMPLETED, RETIRED, WALKOVER, DEFAULTED, a double exit) requires two participants, with one family of exceptions and one rule inside it:

  • The waiver. WALKOVER, DEFAULTED, DOUBLE_WALKOVER or DOUBLE_DEFAULT on a matchUp holding ONE participant is accepted when it is the cascade's own write (propagatingExit), or when the call names no winner, or when the winner it names is awardable. A DOUBLE exit is waived only as the cascade's own write: entered directly it needs both seats reached, because a double exit is one exit per seat (CA, 2026-10-04). Entered beside a seat nobody has reached, the arrival there would meet a double exit already standing, a third entity in a matchUp that holds two.
  • Awardable means the winning side is not the participant already present (a walkover over an opponent nobody knows yet would be two winners of one matchUp), is not a BYE, and is not a phantom: a drawPosition whose assignment exists and holds nobody. An unfilled feed slot, no drawPosition at all, is awardable; so is a QUALIFIER placeholder.
  • A BYE is never the winning side. The participant advances through the BYE and their carried exit occurs where they land. This was decided in two places before it was written here.
  • A policy may switch the requirement off: requireParticipantsForScoring: false.
  • No request flag is involved. The waiver is how a director records a walkover or default before the second opponent arrives (CA, 2026-10-04): "propagateExitStatus shouldn't have anything to do with this ability". propagateExitStatus decides only whether the exit is then carried into the loser's next matchUp. matchUpActions offers it as EXIT.

Before any route, resolveAndApplyOutcome dispatches on two facts: whether downstream is active (something later depends on this result) and whether this matchUp has propagated (it holds a winner or a double exit). A matchUp that has never sent anything downstream cannot invalidate anything there, so a first entry always takes the propagating branch. With both true, a named winner goes to winningSideWithDownstreamDependencies; a directing status or disabled auto-calc goes to applyMatchUpValues; anything else is ERR_NO_VALID_ACTIONS.

A clear of a BYE changes nothing, and is asked before the dispatch: a request with no winner, no score with value and no status but TO_BE_PLAYED, made of a matchUp stored as a BYE, succeeds as noop and still applies its schedule. A BYE has no result to clear, and a TO_BE_PLAYED matchUp with a BYE side never occurs (CA, 2026-10-24; punch-list P26). Until then the clear was accepted and wrote TO_BE_PLAYED against nobody: four seeds of census window sched-w1, on both arms. v1 and v2 ask the one predicate (clearsABye); the harness invariant TO_BE_PLAYED_BESIDE_BYE checks the shape at every census step (aClearedByeStaysABye.test.ts). The lines of a BYE dual are not that shape: the dual is the BYE, and its lines stay TO_BE_PLAYED, as they are generated (CA, 2026-10-24).

The route v2 names (Route, src/mutate/matchUps/outcome/types.ts) is the table's last column for a call that reaches attemptToSetMatchUpStatus; refused is any of its three refusals. The dispatch names six more:

  • swap: allowChangePropagation with a different winner on a matchUp that has one (below).
  • winner: the propagating branch with a winner named, an exit under propagateExitStatus, or a CANCELLED or ABANDONED line that changes its dual's winner: attemptToSetWinningSide.
  • line-score: a line rescored with a winner while its dual loses the winner it had.
  • remove-directed: a score with no winner, or a winner taken away with no score: the directed participants are taken back.
  • clear-score: nothing else matched; the score is removed.
  • apply-values: downstream is active and this matchUp has propagated, with a winner, a directing status or auto-calc disabled (applyMatchUpValues); also a drawn result in a rotating-partner or combined-point round robin with nothing active downstream.

A matchUp in play downstream is active (CA, 2026-10-18, P57): "downstream matchUps e.g. "the match it feeds" should be considered active and a source matchUp (a matchUp earler in the dependency chain) should not be able to be re-scored as unfinished! a downstream matchUp AWAITING_RESULT should be considered an active matchUp also". isActiveDownstream counts a winner or loser target as active when it has a decided result, as before, or when it is in play: its status is IN_PROGRESS, SUSPENDED, AWAITING_RESULT or INCOMPLETE, or its score has a value. A BYE or an exit is not judged by play; its activity remains a question of provenance. So with the final in play, re-scoring a completed semifinal as unfinished is refused the way it is when the final is COMPLETED: IN_PROGRESS, AWAITING_RESULT or a clear is row 9 (ERR_INCOMPATIBLE_MATCHUP_STATUS), a score with no status and no winner is row 15 (ERR_NO_VALID_ACTIONS), and another winner is row 14. A correction that keeps the winner is still accepted, and allowChangePropagation still swaps. Both implementations read the one query (getTargetsDownstream), so v1 and v2 change together. Before 2026-10-18 the re-score was accepted through noDownstreamDependencies, which took the advanced participant back out of the final and left it TO_BE_PLAYED with no score (inPlayDownstreamIsActive.test.ts).

allowChangePropagation with a different winner on a matchUp that has one swaps winner and loser everywhere they have gone (swapWinnerLoser), rather than refusing.

4. What a write does​

modifyMatchUpScore → applyScoreAndStatus is the only place score and matchUpStatus change.

  1. A walkover, or a removal, blanks the result with the toBePlayed fixture: matchUpStatus: TO_BE_PLAYED, score with empty scoreStringSide1 / scoreStringSide2 and sets: undefined, winningSide: undefined, matchUpStatusCodes: [], and matchUpFormat: undefined, though since 7.5.0 a matchUp-level matchUpFormat is carried across the blank (below). The requested status is then written over the blank. Exit provenance survives the blank when the written status is an exit, and BYE claims survive it on a BYE; everything else in sideExitProvenance goes.
  2. Otherwise the given score replaces the old one, then matchUpStatus, matchUpFormat (only if given) and matchUpStatusCodes are written.
  3. Status codes are split at this boundary: the positional array a client sends (['', 'DM']) becomes sideStatusCodes keyed by side, with the exiting side taken from winningSide, not from the index. Only a client's array is split (census A1 residue F1b, 2026-10-18). An engine write whose array is not client input (a propagation in progressExitStatus; the convergence reconcile and the forward writes in doubleExitAdvancement, which re-store the target's own, possibly derived, array; the unwinds in removeDoubleExit) passes derivedStatusCodes, naming the matchUp it describes. Its array is stored as given. It keeps the reasons the matchUp already holds, plus the real reason it carries for the arriving side, on the sides that exited: both sides of a double exit, the losing side of a single exit. A bare WO or DEF that the projection wrote is never a reason. The choice is made by origin, not by value: a client's ['WO'] is still split as its reason. v2 plans only the requested matchUp's own write, from client input, and compares matchUpStatusCodes as display; it plans no sideStatusCodes and no propagation write, so there is nothing to mirror there.
  4. scoredTime is stamped on schedule the first time the matchUp becomes scored (a score with value, a winner, or a completed status), kept through corrections, and deleted when the result is removed. One exception: a DOUBLE_ELIMINATION decider made a DEAD_RUBBER was not played, so it is never stamped and keeps no stamp from a result it held before. A rubber of a team tie is not the exception; its DEAD_RUBBER is stamped like an ABANDONED line (CA, 2026-10-24). It is first-class in NATIVE and has no timeItem mirror.
  5. The schedule the call carried is applied after the outcome is accepted, and was validated before it, so a refusal cannot land on a draw this call has just changed. v2 plans it (S7) where it lands on the matchUp alone, a date, a time, a court, a venue or a court order: v1's own writer is run on a copy of the matchUp, and the schedule it leaves is written (setMatchUpSchedule) before the result, so scoredTime is stamped onto it as v1 stamps it. A schedule v1 refuses, or one carrying another attribute (start, stop, resume and end times, officials), stays v1's.

Decided 2026-10-01 (CA), found by the corpus on real records: rule 1 means a clear normalises rather than restores. On a record whose matchUp had no matchUpStatus and no score, a score-then-clear leaves matchUpStatus: "TO_BE_PLAYED" and an explicit score object; measured on 4 of 11 probed fixtures. That stays: TO_BE_PLAYED and an empty score object are the canonical cleared state, and a reader never sees an undefined status. A clear keeps a matchUp-level matchUpFormat: the format is a property of the match, not of its result. The blank fixture still carries matchUpFormat: undefined, because it is the unwound-matchUp shape, and applyScoreAndStatus now carries the existing format across it the way it carries exit provenance; a call that brings a new format still writes the new one. Pinned by clearKeepsMatchUpFormat.test.ts.

A double exit entered over a completed result (CA, 2026-10-02). A DOUBLE_DEFAULT keeps the score the matchUp had, finished or not, unless the call brings one: both players can be defaulted after the last ball (post-match misconduct at the net, for example), and the score is the record of what was played. The reason belongs in a penalty, addPenalty with each player's participantId and the matchUpId. A DOUBLE_WALKOVER says the match was not played, so it blanks the score; one entered over a completed result is taken at its word, and correcting a mistaken one is the director's re-entry. Neither has a winner. Pinned by authored/outcome-pipeline/double-exit-over-a-completed-result.

5. The side effects, in order​

After the write, and only on success:

  1. Direction. A winner is advanced to the winner target; the loser is directed along the loser link, if the structure has one. A changed result first removes what the previous one directed (removeDirectedParticipants), and that is where ERR_ACTIVE_DRAW_POSITION can arise. A loser taken back leaves every seat their loss reached, a link further on included, and no exit stays awarded to a seat so emptied (census A1, aSeatEmptiedFurtherOnLeavesNoAwardBehind). A first-match loser feed (the link's condition FIRST_MATCHUP) takes the loser only if the match they lost was their first played: no win of their own in the source structure. For a swap (allowChangePropagation with a different winner) the wins counted are those in the rounds before the swapped one. A swap rewrites the advancing drawPosition in every later matchUp, so the new loser loses their later wins with it; counted, those wins would keep out a loser whose first match this was (census w1 9000582 and nine more FMLC seeds, aSwapCountsOnlyTheRoundsBeforeIt; v1's reconcileFedLoserEligibility). A winner directed across a link is seated once (2026-10-22). A winner who already holds a seat in the target matchUp is left there and nothing is written: a direction can arrive twice in one call (a DOUBLE_ELIMINATION Decider seated from the Main final, then the same winner re-advanced by a correction below), and the second used to reach for the other seat and be refused. A placement across a link that fails otherwise, and a winner with no position to take, are not returned: the direction runs after the result is written, and a refusal there would leave that write behind. For a Decider, reconcileDeciders seats it from the final that stands (step 5). Over the suite and the frozen census windows the only placement failures reached were the already-seated case (census de 9304558 with doubleExitPropagateBye: false, census F2 20477784). Pinned by aWinnerAcrossALinkIsSeatedOnce.test.ts.

  2. Exit propagation. WALKOVER, DEFAULTED and (by policy) RETIRED carry into the consolation the loser is fed to; a double exit produces exits downstream. The rules are on exit propagation and are not repeated here. progressExitStatus iterates through up to ten levels of consolation (COMPASS feeds consolation into consolation); a failsafe, not a limit anyone has reached. A relabel keeps the winner the matchUp already has and changes what it says about the loser (CA, 2026-10-02). Re-entered as a WALKOVER or DEFAULTED, it carries the exit to the matchUp the loser already stands in; re-entered as COMPLETED, it withdraws the exit it carried there. Where that matchUp already has a result a director recorded, or the winner of the carried exit has played on from it (counting past any BYEs they were advanced through), the correction is refused before anything is written (row 22, P52): a recorded result downstream fixes whether the origin's outcome is an exit or a played result. The same holds one structure on: where the loser's loss carried their exit into a matchUp it decided for whoever stood opposite, a result that winner has recorded onward counts too (census 20496362). A correction that names the same winner with no status and no score is a played result (COMPLETED), as the write records it, so re-entering a walkover as a bare win changes its kind. An exit carried into a matchUp that another exit already decided for the seat awaiting the loser, with a result recorded below it, is carried past that matchUp as past a BYE: the loser takes the seat, goes on to its winner matchUp, and carries the exit on from there (P51 group 2, CA 2026-10-11). A winner change (a flip) relabels the old loser's place onto the new loser, and their carried exit is re-derived from the corrected result in the same way, so a corrected draw is the one forward play reaches. Both apply whether or not the relabelled matchUp's own winner has played on since. Where the carried exit had converged with another, only this origin's entry is withdrawn and the matchUp re-derives to the kept exit (its carrier directed on, or — a produced exit — won by the relabelled loser, who goes on; a produced exit relayed past a BYE, with nobody in the seat it would be awarded to, stays pending, and the withdrawn side's reason code goes with its exit). Where the kept exit is awarded to the other side than the award that stood, the old winner's advancement is released with it (census S4, DOUBLE_ELIMINATION 20617910); a pending exit stays pending. A double exit re-derived to a single one keeps only its winner's position in the next round: its carrier, now the loser, is released from it (census S4, COMPASS 20586104, doubleExitPropagateBye: false); from a structure's last round that release crosses the link, as a winner's does (DOUBLE_ELIMINATION 20622028). A BYE matchUp left holding only the BYE-status entry a convergence recorded beside it is a plain BYE again. A loser who had won a produced exit on arrival has that award taken back when the origin becomes an exit again, so the exit converges as it would have at first entry (7.7.0; exit propagation).

  3. Round robin tally is recomputed when the matchUp is in a group (updateTallyIfNeeded).

  4. Stale exit origins are reconciled once removals, directions and propagation have all settled: a carried exit whose origin no longer describes an exit is corrected. The reconciliation is asked of the draw as it stands at that point, read afresh, never of a view of the draw taken earlier in the call: such a view describes the draw as the direction left it, before propagation wrote. Steps 4 and 5 run only when the call was accepted (§ 6). Until 2026-10-11 the reconciliation read a deep copy carried in the call's context: what it wrote was lost, and the next call repaired the origin, a refused call included (census family F2).

  5. A mutation OF the decider never triggers it, so a decider played anyway keeps its result; in a TEAM draw a line of the decider's dual is a mutation of the decider (7.8.0). See Double Elimination. Last, no matchUp left without a result keeps its scoredTime (reconcileScoredTimes). It is asked after every settle step, because they can take a result away: settleHeldExits turns an exit holder into a BYE, and the forward settle and the link passes release what was placed. Until 2026-10-24 it was asked before them, so the stamp outlived the result until the next call, whatever that call was: 19 calls over the exitPropagation, drawDefinitions and matchUps suites ended that way (13 BYEs, 6 TO_BE_PLAYED), and none does now (aResultTheSettleRemovesTakesItsScoredTime.test.ts). A result is what § 4 counts as one, so a decider played and then made a DEAD_RUBBER loses its stamp, and a team rubber made a DEAD_RUBBER keeps it.

  6. Notifications (MODIFY_MATCHUP and the draw's topics) are emitted; the factory extension's timeStamp is written by the engine after the call.

    An entity's updatedAt is stamped once per call (F2 (i), CA 2026-10-19): the call's captured instant, or the previous stamp + 1 ms when that is already as late, however many notices the call raises for it. An executionQueue is one call. Until 2026-10-24 v1 stamped at each notice, so one first-round score moved the draw's stamp on by 2 ms (oneStampPerEntityPerCall.test.ts). A state provider without the optional getCapturedStamps/setCapturedStamps hooks keeps no ledger and stamps at each notice, as before.

6. Guarantees​

  • A refusal changes nothing (ERROR_IMPLIES_NO_MUTATION). The checks in § 2 run above every write; the two that used to run late (participants on a bare winner, feed eligibility) were moved up after each was measured leaving a destroyed previous result behind a refusal. Nothing of § 5 after propagation runs on a refused call: no reconciliation, no settling, no scoredTime clean-up. A refusal must not repair, as a side effect, what an earlier call left; that repair is the next accepted call's to make (2026-10-11: the settle step ran after a refusal and was the refused call's only write in 55 of 92 census refusals after a write).

  • A settle step has no failure to report (outcome v2 executor plan, Phase C prerequisite 2; CA, 2026-10-22: "The pipeline needs to work without rollback"). Steps 4 and 5 run after the cascade has written, so an error found there could not be refused without leaving those writes behind, and no caller rollback is relied on. A settle step's inner calls must therefore be unable to fail, and where one could, the failure is checked before the call's first write. Today:

    • reconcileLinkAdvancements cannot fail by construction. releaseLinkedWinnerAdvancement returns nothing or releaseAdvancedDrawPosition's success and declares no error; the pass holds its result in a type that admits none, so a release that one day declares an error does not compile.
    • reconcileScoredTimes cannot fail by construction. Its notice's only error is a missing matchUp, which the pass's guard keeps from reaching it (settlePassesCannotFail.test.ts).
    • reconcileMissedLinkAdvancements returns the error of the direction it makes, and the call is refused on it (CA, 2026-10-24). directWinner can return one there: a target matchUp with no free slot (ERR_EXISTING_POSITION_ASSIGNMENT), or one from the advance it makes onward. It is known only once the cascade has written, and whether a placement will fail depends on yieldSquattingPropagatedBye, which can itself write before it decides, so no pure check can be asked for it in advance. It was dropped until 2026-10-24; it was never reached with an error over the suite and the frozen census windows (2026-10-22), nor over ~97,000 census schedules (settle telemetry, 2026-10-24). A refusal here is an error after a write, accepted because it is not reached; v2 plans it before its first write (aSettlePassReportsWhatItCannotDo.test.ts).

  • A settle loop that reaches its bound with work left refuses the call with SETTLE_BOUND_REACHED, naming the loop (F9 (i), CA 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-24). The bounded loops are propagateExitFrom, sendHeldExitsOn, produceStrandedConvergences, crossLinksThroughByes and reconcileLinkAdvancements; their limits are SETTLE_PASS_LIMITS. Each asks once more whether work is left before refusing, so a draw needing exactly its limit is not refused. None was reached on ~97,000 census schedules; like the dropped error above, a refusal at a bound is an error after a write, accepted because it is not reached, and v2 plans it before its first write (aSettleLoopAtItsBoundRefuses.test.ts). The one exception is the forward settle (settleForwardConsequences): it is a repair loop that a record missing many winner advancements reaches, so it keeps its success with a FORWARD_SETTLE_EXHAUSTED warning naming the matchUp still owed, and the next call settles the rest (CA, 2026-10-24).

    Measured 2026-10-22 over the suite and the frozen census windows w1, w2 and de: the release was reached 4 times and the notice 3,922 times, never with an error.

    What can no longer fail, and why (2026-10-22, CA: an error found after a write is made impossible by construction where that can be shown, and is otherwise handed to v2 as a planned refusal):

    • No positionTargets error after the first write. Its only error is a malformed round link (§ 2 row 21), and row 21 now asks every WINNER and LOSER link in the draw, not only those the matchUp's targets and its downstream walk reach. Nothing that sets an outcome writes a link, so a draw that passes row 21 has none for the rest of the call. Measured on dev 01ce40e544 before the change: a malformed link the walk did not reach was met by settleHeldExits after the score had been written in 57 cells of a scan over five draw types (refusedBeforeTheFirstWrite.test.ts).
    • No addNotes error. notes are validated before the first write (§ 2 row 23).
    • No MISSING_DRAW_POSITIONS from directParticipants. A matchUp with no drawPositions, given a winner on the route that reaches it, is refused before the score is written (§ 2 row 24).
    • A TEAM dual writes nothing before its refusals. Its auto-calc flag and its sides' lineUps were written by handleTeamAutoCalc above every refusal that followed (row 5's AWAITING_RESULT, row 6, row 16); they are now decided there and written once those refusals have been asked.

    Handed to v2 (each is a cascade write whose failure depends on the draw as the cascade leaves it, so v1 cannot ask it before the first write; reached in the suite only by fault injection, and never with an error in the frozen census windows w1, w2 and de, default and doubleExitPropagateBye: false, 2026-10-22): propagateExitFrom (progressExitStatus's placements); the apply-stage refusals inside setMatchUpState after its first write (noDownstreamDependencies, removeDoubleExit, attemptToSetWinningSide's removeDirectedParticipants and directParticipants, attemptToModifyScore's MISSING_ASSIGNMENTS on a line of a dual under manual scoring, updateTieMatchUpScore, relabelWithoutDirection, swapWinnerLoser's writes after its relabel); and in the settle steps, settleRederivedDoubleExits (its propagateUnfillableLoserBye, directWinner and carry), oweDissolvedConvergencesTheirByes, reconcileConsolationReservations, reconcileCarriesPastByes (both calls), settleHeldExits, reconcileByeAdvancements, reconcileDeciders and settleForwardConsequences. They still return their error; none is relied on to be rolled back. Every one of them reaches v2.compare before setMatchUpStatus returns it (outcome v2 executor plan, Phase C prerequisite 3), so under differential a refusal v2 did not plan is a divergence (§ 7.1: the comparison writes back what it captured before v1 ran; v2 itself plans every refusal before its first op, so it has nothing to undo). Until 2026-10-24 the refusals out of settleDraw, settleForwardConsequences and the carry asked after it returned without the comparison (aLateRefusalIsCompared.test.ts).

  • A refused call stores none of a draw's missing edges (CA, 2026-10-18: "no, a refused call does not store a draw's missing edges. v1 should no longer do so."). A draw stored without winnerMatchUpId / loserMatchUpId (an older record, a file the factory did not produce) is given them once, before anything is decided, because the cascade decides on the stored ids. What that write touches (the edges, finishingPositionRange, finishingRound) is held before it and written back on any error the call returns: directly, inside bulkMatchUpStatusUpdate or executionQueue, with or without rollbackOnError, and on the async engine. A call that succeeds keeps them, and the draw stores its edges from then on. v2 refuses before the write is made. Pinned by storedEdgesAreNotRequired.test.ts. A successful call that repairs them announces the draw once, with one MODIFY_DRAW_DEFINITION and no MODIFY_MATCHUP for a matchUp whose only change is an edge (CA, 2026-10-18; anEdgeRepairAnnouncesItsDraw.test.ts).

  • What one step hands the next is the draw itself. A context passed between steps carries references to the stored matchUps, never copies: the engine's deep-copy setting governs what is RETURNED to a caller, never what a mutation reads or writes.

  • Asking for the state a matchUp is already in is satisfied, not repeated. A second identical double exit is a no-op success; without this, the cascade read its own earlier work as a second source and escalated a WALKOVER to a DOUBLE_WALKOVER. Scoped to double exits only.

  • A first entry always propagates. Dispatching on "downstream active" alone refused a TO_BE_PLAYED Main matchUp above a played consolation and, routed the other way, silently stopped winners advancing in 27 FMLC cells.

  • Score strings never disagree with sets, because they are not accepted.

  • A completed matchUp with a valid winning score cannot be reverted to a live status without a new result; submit a corrected outcome or clear first.

  • The properties the exit-propagation harness checks (DO_UNDO_IDENTITY, IDEMPOTENT_REAPPLY, MONOTONIC_DECISION) are stated in the corpus vocabulary (corpus/INVARIANTS.md) and measured on the generated fixtures in the matrix and census. These are intended invariants, not a universal proof. On real records, see § 8 (real-record do/undo) and the clear that normalises in § 4.

7. Write mode​

In NATIVE mode (the default since 5.0.0), schedule stamps such as scoredTime use first-class matchUp.schedule attributes. LEGACY uses the corresponding timeItems; BRIDGE writes both via setFirstClassOrTimeItem. Result, status and scoring codes remain first-class.

This storage mapping is not evidence that exit behavior is identical across modes. Provenance writes are mode-gated and propagation decisions read provenance; a NATIVE census result must not be reported as LEGACY/BRIDGE validation. Cross-mode compatibility requires its own fixtures and replay evidence. See harness measurement limits.

7.1 Two implementations (S2)​

src/mutate/matchUps/outcome/ is the clean-room re-implementation of this page, written from it and from the corpus. It works over a read-only view of the draw: refuseOutcome(request, view) is § 2 as one pure function, chooseRoute is § 3, planWrite is § 4 and planDirection is § 5's direction and exit propagation. v1 is the pipeline as written across setMatchUpState and the matchUpGovernor routes, and it is still the only one that writes.

The mode is process-wide, set with engine.outcomePipeline(mode) and read with engine.getOutcomePipeline(); no argument restores v1, and an unknown mode is INVALID_VALUES.

  • v1 (the default): nothing is built and nothing is decided by v2.

  • v2: v2 decides the refusals. A refusal it raises is the call's result and v1 is not asked. An outcome it accepts is planned (§ 7.2); a call whose complete plan is a shape v2 owns is written by v2 (outcome/apply.ts: the plan's ops folded into the stored draw by the one reducer, then v1's own notices for what was written) and v1 is not asked; every other call is handed to v1 whole, which routes and writes as under v1. Since S2 v2 owns the local write: the matchUp's own result (an in-progress, suspended or corrected live score, a format change with it, a cleared live score, or the same result submitted again, which changes nothing but stamps), with no winner before or after, no exit, nothing directed, no tally and no settle work. It leaves the record, the notices and the result v1 leaves for the same call (ownedLocalWrites.test.ts, under a fixed clock); an owned call takes no hydration, and its updateInContextMatchUp is built only for a subscriber, after the call has succeeded. Since S3 v2 also owns the first direction: a result naming a winner, not an exit, whose winner goes into the next matchUp of the same structure (holding no exit or result) and whose loser, if a link takes them, arrives on a free seat carrying nothing, with no settle work after. A loser placed opposite a BYE goes on through it at once, as v1 sends them (advanceIntoWinnerMatchUp): their position joins the next matchUp of that structure, or occupies it there when it was advanced structurally while their seat stood empty, and that matchUp is announced. Only a clean next matchUp is planned (TO_BE_PLAYED, no BYE, exit, carry, provenance or result, no earlier advance of the BYE's position to replace, and no FIRST_MATCHUP reservation it would then raise). A winner whose next matchUp holds a BYE (a consolation seat a reservation filled) goes on past it the same way, under the same conditions. An ad hoc matchUp's winner directs nothing, and its structure's tally is planned when it holds one round (a Swiss's later rounds stay v1's). A result in a lucky draw's pre-feed round (a round of odd size) directs nothing either: v1 advances no one from it, the advancement being chosen afterwards (luckyDrawAdvancement). An FMLC loser fed into the consolation's round 2 is included (the seat positionTargets reports is the one directLoser takes). A loser that feed keeps out (they have won before) is owned too: their seat takes a propagated BYE and its matchUp becomes a BYE, as directLoser places it, or nothing is written when a reservation made the seat a BYE already; a BYE that would meet somebody in the other seat stays v1's. A seeded loser stays v1's: v1 carries the seed into the structure they are placed in (propagateLoserSeed), which is not yet an op. It writes v1's notices for it (the next matchUp's modifyMatchUp named assignMatchUpDrawPosition, a modifyPositionAssignments for the structure the loser is placed in) and returns v1's direction context (ownedDirectionWrites.test.ts). Under v2 the mutations suite has about 10,000 calls v2 writes as a direction. A seat already a BYE needs nothing. A participant carrying their own walkover or default into a seat beside a BYE goes through it, and v1 writes their carry entry (the exit and the matchUp it came from) on the side of the BYE matchUp they passed, as well as where they land. It does so whether the carrier was seated first and the BYE arrived after them, or the BYE stood there first; before this, the BYE-first order wrote the entry only in a structure's last round, so the same results entered in the two orders left two draws. v2 reads that entry on the BYE matchUp as an exit standing there, as it reads any carried entry, and plans a convergence with it when an arriving exit meets it. A BYE matchUp written before this, BYE first and outside the last round, holds only the BYE claim; for it v2 still reads the carry where the carrier landed, a round on. One carry counts once: an entry where the carrier landed that names an origin the BYE matchUp already records is not read a second time. An entry left beside a BYE whose carrier's seat has since gone to a BYE, because a correction made the carrier's origin a double exit, is not a held exit, and the settle step does not send it on; only that double exit's produced exit travels from there.

What a divergence leaves behind: nothing. An error changes nothing (2026-10-18; a refused call stores none of a draw's missing edges either, § 6), and a divergence is an error raised after v1 has written: v2 plans and never writes, v1 runs and writes, and only then does the comparison throw. So before v1 runs, differential mode captures what the call can touch (the tournament record, the draw and the event), the notice buffer and the mutation flag, and before the divergence leaves setMatchUpStatus it writes them back into the same objects. The record is then exactly as it was before the call, on every path:

  • with or without rollbackOnError, directly or inside executionQueue, and on the async engine;
  • the result is { error } carrying the divergence message, and nothing is notified;
  • the record is not stamped as modified (a state provider without the optional setMutationStatus has its flag cleared instead, as a rollback clears it);
  • under devContext the divergence is rethrown, over the same unchanged record.

Pinned by divergenceLeavesBehind.test.ts. The capture is taken only in differential mode; v1 and v2 take none. A divergence is still a failing signal, not a result to build on: the suite's rethrowing provider fails the test, and the exit-propagation census records it as NO_EXCEPTION_ESCAPES. Inside a bulkMatchUpStatusUpdate, the outcomes applied before the one that diverged stay written unless the call asked for rollbackOnError, the same as for any refusal a later outcome meets (§ 8).

A TEAM line is owned in two shapes (S7): a line's result that leaves its dual undecided, and one that decides it. The line's result is any first result with a winner: scored, a winner alone (no score), or a single exit (WALKOVER, DEFAULTED, RETIRED with its score), each counted for its winner in the dual's tally. A line's double exit (DOUBLE_WALKOVER, DOUBLE_DEFAULT) is written as it stands (only-score): it counts for neither side, the dual is tallied without it, and nothing is produced or carried (a line's targets are its dual's; v1's doubleExitAdvancement does not run for it). v1 writes the line (as any result: modifyMatchUpScore, its scoredTime), then updateTieMatchUpScore writes the dual:

  1. it ensures the dual's side line-ups;
  2. it copies the resolved tieFormat onto the dual;
  3. it tallies the dual's score from its lines;
  4. it derives the dual's status: COMPLETED with a winner, else IN_PROGRESS where isActiveMatchUp reads it active, else TO_BE_PLAYED.

v2 plans the line's write and the dual's (setMatchUpResult with dualTally, no scoredTime):

  • the projected score and status;
  • the sides ensuredDualSides gives (the rule ensureSideLineUps writes by, one function) and the copied tieFormat, each only where it changes the stored value. On a dual's first line both change, and the dual goes from TO_BE_PLAYED to IN_PROGRESS; a record that stored no tieFormat of its own takes it on the same way.

Both are announced as v1 announces them: each matchUp's modifyMatchUp and its in-context notice, the line's first. The call returns the dual's write as tieMatchUpResult, as v1's directParticipants does. A line directs nothing of its own: its targets are its dual's.

A line played on a dual already decided (the remaining lines of a COMPLETED dual) is written the same way: the line, then the dual's tally, the dual staying COMPLETED and keeping its winner. Nothing is directed or carried again, since the dual's winner does not change; the next dual is left as it is, played or not. A dual walked over with its lines already at the value goal's edge is written the same way when a line takes it there (COMPLETED, its winner kept).

A line that decides its dual makes the dual's own call: v1's directParticipants writes the projected winner and score onto the dual (COMPLETED) and directs the dual as any winner is directed. v2 plans that call as it plans any call, from a view of the dual taken on the line's own index (no hydration): routed, written and directed by the same functions a singles result is (decidedDualCall). Its write is the dual's tally (no status codes, no scoredTime, as updateTieMatchUpScore writes it). Once the winner's position has joined the next dual, the winning team's line-up is carried onto its side there (setSideLineUp, v1's propagateLineUp):

  • the line-up the decided dual's winning side holds, without substitutions (removeLineUpSubstitutions, which regroups each player's assignments);
  • on the side v1 reckons by roundPosition: a feed round's advancing team, or one advanced by position, is side 2.

The next dual is announced by directWinner, and the call's tieMatchUpResult carries the dual's winner. A final carries nothing on.

Where a loser link sends the losing team on (a consolation, a compass direction), the loser is placed as any loser is, and the losing team's line-up is carried onto its side of the dual it lands in (setSideLineUp, v1's propagateLoserLineUp):

  • the line-up the decided dual's losing side held before the line's write, every assignment kept (the winner's drops any that names no participant), without substitutions;
  • on side 2 - (roundPosition % 2) of a first-round dual, by the decided dual's roundPosition; on side 1 of a later round's.

That dual is announced by directLoser. A loser kept out of a first-match feed (a BYE takes the seat) carries nothing. A loser seated opposite a BYE carries its line-up onto the BYE matchUp as above and goes on through it into the dual beyond, whose side takes the team's line-up as the draw holds it (setSideLineUp, v1's updateSideLineUp at the loser's assignment), on the side holding the loser's drawPosition, announced updateSidLineUp before the BYE matchUp's directLoser. A team the draw holds no line-up for takes none there, as v1 writes it.

A dual's own call given a winner (scored or not, or a single exit) is written and directed as any winner is (directParticipants): the result with its status codes and scoredTime, the winner placed in the next dual (announced assignMatchUpDrawPosition). Before the result v1 writes the dual's sides (ensureSideLineUps): setMatchUpResult carries them as ensuredSides, where they change, and the call returns no tieMatchUpResult. Nothing is carried from the dual: its winner's next dual takes no line-up. Where a loser link places the losing team, its assignment writes the team's line-up as the draw holds it on its side of each dual it reaches (setSideLineUp, updateSideLineUp, announced updateSidLineUp): past a BYE the dual beyond first, then the BYE matchUp. The loser's own dual is announced with no event (assignTeamLineUp hands updateSideLineUp none).

A dual's own exit carried across its loser link (propagateExitStatus) is written on the dual the losing team reaches as any carried exit is, by that dual's own call (setMatchUpState), whose ensureSideLineUps first writes its sides with the team arrived: the carried setMatchUpResult takes them as ensuredSides. That write comes after the team's line-up is written there, so the dual is announced as the carried result is (modifyMatchUpScore, with its event). Planned where both the reached dual's sides hold their drawPositions; a carry that goes on past the dual it reaches (its own loser link, as COMPASS's West), through a BYE, or onto a DOUBLE_ELIMINATION losers' dual its arrival half places (hydrated unnumbered, read numbered by v1 after the arrival) stays v1's.

A dual's own double exit (DOUBLE_WALKOVER or DOUBLE_DEFAULT, on a dual still to be played) is written and advanced as any double exit is (doubleExitAdvancement): the exit it produces in the next dual, awarded to the side opposite or waiting for it, and the BYEs on the seats its loser links feed. Its sides are written first, as for a winner (ensuredSides). A double exit over a dual in progress stays v1's (§ 7.1's exit rules).

A dual's own call on a dual already decided (by its lines or by its own call) is planned as any decided matchUp's is: its winner entered again stands where it stood, a changed winner is re-directed. Its sides are written first (ensuredSides). A winner's assignment writes the team's line-up, as the draw holds it, on its side of the next dual wherever that dual already sides its drawPosition (assignTeamLineUp → updateSideLineUp, a setSideLineUp announced updateSidLineUp with no event): so a winner entered again replaces the line-up its deciding line carried there, and a first entry writes none. A changed winner over a dual's own single exit stays v1's (settle).

A line's result removed, or a line cleared (remove-directed, clear-score), or corrected (winner over a stored winner, either side), is written as a first result is: the line rewritten, then the dual's tally re-read from its remaining lines, where the dual's winner does not change (a dual still undecided, or decided by its other lines) and the dual still holds a line's result. The line directs nothing, so nothing is taken back from the next dual, and a correction is neither a re-entered nor a changed winner. v1 clears it through removeDirectedParticipants, which returns no tieMatchUpResult, and neither does v2 (dualTally.unreturned).

Enabling also takes the winner and score the dual's lines project (generateTieMatchUpScore), whatever winner the call names.

A line that un-decides its dual (S7, the line-score route: a decided dual whose lines, with this one in place, project no winner) is planned as v1 writes it (scoreModification): the dual's direction taken back first (removeDirectedParticipants through the line: the winner's position out of the next dual, announced first), then the line, then the dual's tally.
