Repeated overtime, exact extra-time halves and finite ceilings

The overtime count is independent of the regulation count. Four quarters with up to three overtimes means four regulation segments and at most three additional periods, not a best-of-seven match.

Availability

Overtime codes the scoring engine scores are valid for tournament use in Factory releases after 7.29.0 (CA 2026-11-04); a match clock with overtime, and untimed or tiebreak-bearing periods, are not.

Repeated overtime without a ceiling​

QTR4XA-S:T12P-O:U/T5P

Play four twelve-minute quarters and compare aggregate points. If tied, play a five-minute overtime. Compare at its end; if still tied, play another. U is unlimited repetition, not a large hidden cap. A lead during a period does not finish ordinary overtime.

The NBA examples include games with two and four overtimes.

Finite ceilings ending level​

PER3XA-S:T20P-O:1SD/T5P

Play three twenty-minute regulation periods. If tied, offer one five-minute sudden-death period. A goal ends it immediately; no goal by the ceiling finishes the covered play level. Historical NHL regular-season ties provide real examples.

Without SD, the ceiling still applies, but a lead is assessed at the period end:

HAL2XA-S:T45P-O:2/T15P

There are at most two additional fifteen-minute periods. If one side leads after the first, finish there. This expresses the period-boundary behavior of a historical silver goal.

Exact extra-time halves​

HAL2XA-S:T45P-O:2X/T15P

If regulation is tied, play both extra-time halves. Compare the accumulated score only when both are complete. A lead after the first half does not waive the second.

In Belgium–USA at the 2014 World Cup, regulation was 0–0. Belgium scored twice in the first extra half; the USA scored in the second. The result is 2–1, not an early 2–0 finish. See the football examples.

X means exactly the stated number of periods in one block. It does not mean repeated mandatory pairs. A grammar for repeating whole blocks is a separate extension.

A final fallback after the ceiling​

PER3XA-S:T20P-O:3/T5P-F:TB1

If still tied after three overtimes, play a one-point final decider. Its segment number is 3 regulation + 3 overtime + 1 = 7. It is not segment 4 simply because regulation contained three periods. If overtime produces a winner earlier, the fallback is not played.

This is a format illustration, not a claim that an NHL shootout is a one-point tiebreak. See shootout handoffs.

Combinations that do not make sense​

CombinationProblem
UXUnlimited repetition cannot also be an exact finite block
XSDMandatory completion of every period conflicts with immediate sudden death
U with FAn unlimited while-tied sequence never reaches a finite fallback
O without AThis overtime extension resolves aggregate totals

The relationship between a match-wide M clock and overtime is not yet defined. Do not infer that a match clock includes or excludes extra time. Likewise, the first implementation supports timed regulation and overtime without segment-local tiebreaks; readable segment syntax is not a promise of every scoring combination.
