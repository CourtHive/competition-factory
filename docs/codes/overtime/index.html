Overtime and extra-time formats

Overtime is additional play offered when regulation finishes with aggregate scores level. A matchUpFormat code should say how long each extra period lasts, whether to repeat, when to compare totals, and what happens if the scores are still level at the limit.

Availability

Overtime codes the scoring engine scores are valid for tournament use in Factory releases after 7.29.0 (CA 2026-11-04); a match clock with overtime, and untimed or tiebreak-bearing periods, are not.

The overtime section: -O:​

{ROOT}{count}[X][A]-S:{segment}[-G:{game}][-O:{limit}[X][SD]/{segment}][-F:{segment}][-M:{constraint}]

The new section is O for overtime. It follows G, if present, and precedes F and M. The O in -O: is distinct from an outs-based segment such as -S:O3.

PartMeaningExample
limitMaximum additional periods, excluding regulation and the final deciderO:3/T5P
URepeat without a numerical ceiling while tiedO:U/T5P
XPlay exactly this many overtime periods before comparing totalsO:2X/T15P
SDSudden death: end immediately when a scoring event breaks the tieO:1SD/T5P
/segmentReuse an existing segment specificationT5P = five minutes, scored in points

O requires aggregate scoring, declared with A. Regulation and overtime points accumulate into the same sporting total. Winning more individual periods does not decide an aggregate match.

Regulation → overtime → final decider​

Play every regulation segment. An unequal aggregate ends the match. A level aggregate enters overtime. Ordinary overtime is compared at period ends; exact blocks wait until the whole block ends; sudden death can end during a period.

At a finite ceiling, an unequal aggregate decides the match. If still level, enter a declared F fallback, or finish level when there is none. The fallback's deciding score is separate from the sporting aggregate. Unlimited overtime has no reachable F fallback.

Choose the behavior you mean​

  • Repeated overtime, exact extra-time halves and finite ceilings: U versus a cap versus X.
  • Sudden death, golden goals and silver goals: a scoring event versus a period boundary.
  • Shootout handoffs and tied results: finishing level, final deciders and separate shootout scores.

Worked sporting examples​

The examples use published results rather than scores invented to fit a format:

  • Basketball: repeated overtime, including two NBA games that required four overtimes.
  • Hockey: sudden death and finite ceilings, including a four-overtime playoff game and historical draws.
  • Football: exact halves, golden goals and silver goals, including an extra-time lead that must not end play early.
