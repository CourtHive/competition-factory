Shootout handoffs and tied results

A format must distinguish finishing level, playing a final decider, and handing off to a shootout. Those outcomes should not be hidden inside an ambiguous overtime count.

Availability

Overtime codes the scoring engine scores are valid for tournament use in Factory releases after 7.29.0 (CA 2026-11-04); a match clock with overtime, and untimed or tiebreak-bearing periods, are not. A full shootout attempt model is a separate part of the scoring design.

Finishing level at a finite ceiling​

PER3XA-S:T20P-O:1SD/T5P

If the single overtime finishes scoreless, the covered match finishes level without a winner. That was a real NHL regular-season outcome before shootouts were introduced. The historical hockey examples include 1–1 and 3–3 final results.

Exactly-N regulation can also finish level without any overtime. For example, SET2X-S:6/TB7 means play exactly two sets; 1–1 is a valid finish. Plain SET2 means “best of two” and is not an alternative way to request exact play or overtime.

A final decider is separate from the aggregate​

PER3XA-S:T20P-O:3/T5P-F:TB1

F offers a final one-point decider if the aggregate is still tied at the overtime ceiling. The decider settles the winner; its score is not added into the regulation-plus-overtime aggregate.

Without O, an existing XA format's F is the decider immediately after regulation. With O, F follows the finite overtime ceiling. An unlimited overtime sequence cannot also have a reachable F fallback.

Shootout handoffs are not F:TB1​

A shootout can require alternating attempts, equal opportunities, early mathematical elimination, and further attempts when still level. A one-point tiebreak does not describe those rules.

The sourced tests therefore stop at the overtime boundary for three shootout games. They retain the actual eventual winner as a separate fact; they do not assert that the present overtime reducer can produce that winner through a fabricated one-point fallback.

A shootout decider: F:SO{rounds}​

HAL2XA-S:T45P-O:2X/T15P-F:SO5

SO5 is a shootout of five rounds, the final segment after a level overtime. It needs -O:: a match that goes straight to penalties (SET2XA-S:T45P-F:SO5) is not a code; its shootout is recorded on the result (score.overtime.decidedIn: 'EXTERNAL', decidedBy: { method: 'SHOOTOUT', tally }). It is entered as its final tally, never attempt by attempt: France–Switzerland above is 3–3 through extra time and 4–5 in the shootout. A tally is refused when no shootout ends on it: level, past the five rounds by more than one (sudden death is won by one), or more than the shootout could reach before one side could no longer catch up (4–0 in five rounds; it ends at 3–0). Attempt-by-attempt entry is a later slice that reduces to the same tally.

An NHL shootout-winner goal in the published final is not a goal scored during overtime. Football's penalty tally is also separate from the goals scored during the match. Preserving that distinction is essential for standings, statistics, and explaining how a match was decided.
