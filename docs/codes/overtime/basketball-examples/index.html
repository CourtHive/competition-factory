Basketball examples — repeated overtime

NBA overtime provides clear examples of repeated overtime without a numerical ceiling. A lead during an overtime period is not enough: play continues until the period ends.

Availability

Overtime codes the scoring engine scores are valid for tournament use in Factory releases after 7.29.0 (CA 2026-11-04); a match clock with overtime, and untimed or tiebreak-bearing periods, are not.

QTR4XA-S:T12P-O:U/T5P

Four twelve-minute regulation quarters; if the total is tied, repeat five-minute overtime periods until one ends with unequal totals. See NBA Rule 5, sections II and IV.

Double and quadruple overtime results​

Scores use the named team order, away then home. Overtime entries are period scores, not running totals. The linked NBA pages publish every period score and the final result.

Why U matters​

New York–Atlanta remained tied after its first, second and third overtimes. A hidden one-, two-, or three-period ceiling would stop before the published result. U allows the fourth period to be played.

X would be different: O:4X/T5P would require all four extra periods even if the first ended unequal. SD would also be different: the first overtime score would end the match. Neither describes these games.

What the result tests check​

Offline fixtures retain every regulation and overtime period, not just the final score. Tests reproduce each result from period-total input and verify that every earlier period leaves the match in progress. Point-input parity uses a synthetic order consistent with each published period total; it does not claim to reproduce historical possession-by-possession play.
